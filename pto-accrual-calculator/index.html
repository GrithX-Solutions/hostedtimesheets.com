How to derive an hourly accrual rate

If a policy states an annual PTO allowance, a simple hourly rate can be estimated as annual PTO hours ÷ annual eligible work hours. For example, 80 PTO hours across 2,080 eligible work hours equals about 0.03846 PTO hours per eligible hour.

Convert an annual PTO allowance to a pay-period accrual

If a policy grants a fixed annual allowance, divide the annual PTO hours by the number of eligible pay periods. For example, 120 PTO hours across 26 biweekly pay periods equals about 4.615 PTO hours per eligible pay period before policy-specific rounding.

PTO per pay period = annual PTO allowance ÷ eligible pay periods

Accruing PTO from hours worked

If you need an accruing PTO calculator for hourly employees, use the hours-worked accrual method and enter the eligible hours plus the PTO earned per eligible hour. The calculator multiplies those values and then applies any carryover, usage and balance cap you enter.

Accrued PTO = eligible hours worked × PTO hours earned per eligible hour

Convert PTO hours to days

To convert a PTO balance from hours to workdays, divide PTO hours by the employee's normal hours per workday.

PTO days = PTO hours ÷ hours per workday

Example: 80 PTO hours ÷ 8 hours per workday = 10 PTO days. If a normal workday is 7.5 hours, the same 80-hour balance is about 10.67 workdays.

Methodology and assumptions

This calculator performs arithmetic from the policy values you enter. It does not decide which hours are eligible, whether a cap applies, whether unused time carries over, or which legal rules govern a plan. Use the actual employer or plan terms for those inputs.

The calculator uses the entered numeric values without an internal policy-rounding rule. It displays earned PTO and projected balances to two decimal places; the hourly-rate formula line displays the accrual rate to four decimal places. If a policy rounds accrual at a specific step, apply that documented rule to the inputs or reconcile the result against the authoritative HR or payroll record.

How PTO accrual is calculated

PTO policies commonly accrue time in one of several ways: by hours worked, as a fixed number of hours each pay period, or as another fixed allocation.

You can also use this tool as a vacation accrual calculator or time-off accrual calculator when the policy uses the same hourly, pay-period or fixed-allocation math.

After calculating earned time, use the Time Off Tracker to organize PTO, vacation, sick and personal leave balances.

Per-hour accrual

PTO earned = hours worked × accrual rate

If you work 160 hours and earn 0.05 PTO hours for each hour worked, you accrue 8 hours of PTO.

Per-pay-period accrual

PTO earned = number of pay periods × PTO hours per pay period

This is useful when a policy grants a consistent amount on each paycheck rather than tying accrual directly to hours worked.

Fixed accrual

PTO earned = fixed PTO hours entered

Use the fixed method when the policy value you want to model is already a PTO-hour amount rather than a rate tied to work hours or pay periods.

Projected balance

Projected balance = current balance + carryover + PTO earned − PTO used

The calculator applies a positive balance cap to the final projected balance after carryover, new accrual and used PTO are combined. It does not stop accrual when the balance reaches a cap; it only limits the displayed projection to the cap you enter.

If PTO used is greater than the current balance, carryover and newly earned PTO combined, the calculator can show a negative projected balance. That is an arithmetic result only; the tool does not decide whether a policy permits negative PTO or borrowing against future accrual.

Frequently asked questions

What accrual rate should I enter?

Use the rate stated in your employer's PTO policy or plan. This calculator does not determine which rate applies to you.

Does this calculate PTO according to state or country law?

No. It performs arithmetic using the policy values you enter. Legal requirements and employer policies can differ by location and circumstance.

What does a PTO cap mean?

A cap is the maximum balance allowed under a policy. When a cap is entered, this calculator limits the projected balance to that value.

How do I calculate PTO accrual based on hours worked?

Multiply eligible hours worked by the PTO accrual rate. For example, 160 eligible hours × 0.05 PTO hours per hour = 8 PTO hours earned.

How do I convert PTO hours to workdays?

Divide the PTO balance in hours by the employee's normal hours per workday. For example, 80 PTO hours ÷ 8 hours per workday = 10 workdays.

How much PTO accrues per biweekly pay period?

Divide the annual PTO allowance by the number of eligible biweekly pay periods when the policy grants PTO evenly by pay period. For example, 120 hours ÷ 26 pay periods is about 4.615 hours per eligible pay period before policy-specific rounding.

Can the projected PTO balance be negative?

Yes. If PTO used is greater than the current balance, carryover and newly earned PTO combined, the calculator can show a negative arithmetic balance. Whether a policy permits negative PTO is a separate policy question.

PTO balance planning

Model the accrual method before projecting the balance

The calculator supports hourly, per-pay-period and fixed accrual inputs. Use the method and values from the actual PTO policy rather than choosing the option that produces the expected balance.

Hourly accrual is driven by eligible hours

With an hourly method, PTO earned equals eligible hours multiplied by the accrual rate. At 0.05 PTO hours per eligible work hour, 160 eligible hours earn 8 PTO hours. Whether overtime, holidays or paid leave count as eligible hours depends on the policy and is not decided by the calculator.

Per-pay-period accrual uses an amount, not hours worked

If a plan adds 4 hours every eligible pay period, the accrual is based on the number of qualifying periods you enter. A biweekly schedule and a semimonthly schedule have different numbers of periods per year, so confirm the actual payroll frequency when converting an annual allowance.

Carryover, usage and caps affect the projected balance

Start with the current balance, add new accrual and approved carryover, then subtract PTO used. The calculator applies any positive cap to that final arithmetic balance. Policies can instead stop accrual at a cap, discard excess time or handle the limit another way, so use the tool to model the numbers rather than choose the policy rule.

Scenario example

An employee begins with 18 hours, earns 6 hours during the period, receives 4 hours of approved carryover and uses 8 hours. Before any cap, the projected balance is 20 hours: 18 + 6 + 4 − 8. If a policy cap were 16, the policy would determine how the excess is handled.

Use the result as a reconciliation aid

Compare the rate, eligible-hours basis, pay-period count, opening balance, usage, carryover, cap and rounding method with payroll or HR records. For a detailed derivation of accrual rates, see How to Calculate PTO Accrual.
