Quick answer

A military time converter changes the same clock time between compact four-digit military notation, the colon-form 24-hour clock, and the 12-hour AM/PM clock.

1730 military = 17:30 = 5:30 PM

The minutes do not change. The hour changes only when you cross noon or midnight.

How to convert military time to regular time

Read the first two digits as the 24-hour hour and the last two digits as minutes. For hours from 13 through 23, subtract 12 and use PM. Hours from 01 through 11 stay the same and use AM.

Military hour 13–23 → subtract 12 → PM

Example: 1730 military time

Split 1730 into hour 17 and minute 30. Subtract 12 from 17 to get 5, keep the 30 minutes, and use PM. The result is 5:30 PM.

Example: 0830 military time

0830 is before noon, so the hour stays 8 and the minutes stay 30. The result is 8:30 AM.

How to convert regular time to military time

For 1:00 PM through 11:59 PM, add 12 to the hour. For 1:00 AM through 11:59 AM, keep the hour and add a leading zero when needed. Noon and midnight are the two special cases.

7:45 PM → 7 + 12 = 19 → 1945

7:45 AM → 0745

Midnight and noon

This converter uses 0000 for midnight and 1200 for noon. It accepts military values from 0000 through 2359. It deliberately rejects 2400 so the input always maps to one time within a single 24-hour day.

Military time versus the 24-hour clock

This page displays military-style time as four digits without a colon, such as 1730, and the equivalent 24-hour clock as 17:30. The underlying hour and minute are the same; only the notation changes.

Smart input rules

The converter accepts compact military values such as 730 or 1730, colon-form 24-hour values such as 07:30 or 17:30, and standard values such as 7:30 AM or 5:30 PM. It also recognizes the words noon and midnight. Hours must stay within the selected notation and minutes must be from 00 through 59.

What this converter does not do

This tool changes clock notation only. It does not calculate time duration, convert time zones, apply UTC offsets, or account for daylight-saving changes. Use the Time Duration Calculator for the span between two times and the Clock Out Time Calculator when you need a planned leaving time.

Why military time helps with schedules

A 24-hour clock removes the AM/PM label, which can reduce ambiguity in shift schedules, time cards, travel times, medical logs, operations, and other time-sensitive records. For work-hours math, keep the time format separate from the duration calculation.

Methodology and assumptions

The parser converts every accepted input to an hour from 0 through 23 and a minute from 0 through 59. The page then formats that same value as four-digit military time, colon-form 24-hour time, and 12-hour AM/PM time. The current-time button reads your device clock; no time value is sent to a server.

Frequently asked questions

What is 1730 in regular time?

1730 military time is 5:30 PM. The hour 17 is greater than 12, so subtract 12 and keep the minutes: 17:30 → 5:30 PM.

How do I convert PM time to military time?

For 1:00 PM through 11:59 PM, add 12 to the hour and keep the minutes. For example, 7:45 PM becomes 1945. Noon stays 1200.

What is midnight in military time?

This converter uses 0000 for midnight and 1200 for noon. It accepts military values from 0000 through 2359.

Does military time use a colon?

Military-style time on this page is shown as four digits without a colon, such as 1730. The equivalent 24-hour clock display is shown separately as 17:30.

Can I enter 7:30 PM instead of military time?

Yes. The smart input accepts 12-hour AM/PM time, colon-form 24-hour time, or compact military time and shows all three formats.

Does this converter change time zones?

No. It converts clock notation only. It does not convert cities, UTC offsets, daylight-saving rules, or local time zones.

Does this converter accept 2400?

No. To avoid date-boundary ambiguity, this converter uses the range 0000 through 2359 and represents midnight as 0000.

Fast conversion rules

Four rules cover nearly every conversion

Keep the minutes. Change only the hour and AM/PM label.

Morning: keep the hour and pad it

For 1:00 AM through 11:59 AM, use the same hour in military time and add a leading zero below 10. Example: 6:20 AM becomes 0620.

Afternoon and evening: add 12

For 1:00 PM through 11:59 PM, add 12 to the hour. Example: 9:15 PM becomes 2115.

Noon stays 1200

12:00 PM is 1200. Do not add another 12 to noon.

Midnight becomes 0000

12:00 AM is 0000 in this converter. Times after midnight continue as 0001, 0015, 0100, and so on.
