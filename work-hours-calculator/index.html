Quick answer

This free work hours calculator finds net worked time from a start time, end time and unpaid break.

Net hours per day = elapsed shift time − unpaid break

Total work hours = net hours per day × workdays

Estimated gross pay = total work hours × hourly rate

Example: 9:00 AM to 5:30 PM is 8.5 elapsed hours. Subtract a 30-minute unpaid break and the net workday is 8 hours. Across 5 identical workdays, total work time is 40 hours.

The optional hourly-rate field multiplies total calculated work hours by the rate you enter. The result is a simple gross-pay estimate before overtime premiums, taxes, deductions or employer-specific payroll rules.

Methodology and assumptions

The calculator uses the exact start and end minutes you enter; it does not apply time-clock rounding. It finds elapsed shift time, treats an earlier end time as the following day, subtracts unpaid break minutes, floors the daily result at zero, and then multiplies that net daily result by the number of repeated workdays. Identical start and end times return zero. Use the time-card calculator instead when daily schedules vary.

How to calculate hours worked

Subtract the start time from the end time, then subtract unpaid break minutes. If you work the same schedule on multiple days, multiply the daily worked time by the number of days.

Example

9:00 AM to 5:30 PM is 8.5 elapsed hours. Subtract a 30-minute unpaid lunch and the worked time is 8.0 hours.

Overnight shifts

A shift such as 10:00 PM to 6:00 AM crosses midnight. The calculator automatically treats the end time as the following day.

If you only need the duration between two times without repeated-workday or payroll logic, use the Time Duration Calculator.

Repeated schedule vs. changing shifts

The Workdays field is best when the same start, end and break repeat across several days. If each day has different punches, use the Time Card Calculator instead so each day is recorded separately.

How the pay estimate works

Estimated pay is total calculated hours multiplied by the hourly rate you enter. The calculator uses one rate for all repeated workdays and does not add taxes, deductions, overtime premiums, shift differentials or employer-specific payroll adjustments.

Check important records

For payroll or billing, compare the result with the original time record and the rounding or break rules that actually apply. The calculator performs time arithmetic; it does not determine employment policy.

Work-hours math

Use elapsed time, break time and workdays as separate inputs

This calculator is designed for a repeated shift pattern. It calculates one net workday first, then multiplies that result by the number of workdays you enter.

Start with elapsed shift time

Convert the start and end times into one continuous duration. A 9:00 AM to 5:30 PM shift is 8 hours 30 minutes elapsed. If the end time is earlier than the start time, the calculator treats the shift as crossing midnight into the next day.

Subtract unpaid breaks after finding the elapsed shift

For a 9:00 AM–5:30 PM shift with a 30-minute unpaid lunch, net worked time is 8 hours. Use the break field only for time that should be excluded from worked time. If a separate timekeeping record already removes an off-the-clock meal period, avoid deducting it a second time.

Multiply only when the schedule really repeats

If the same 8-hour net shift occurs on five days, the weekly result is 40 hours. If start times, end times or breaks change from day to day, calculate each day separately instead of multiplying one assumed schedule. The weekly or time-card calculators are better suited to variable schedules.

Read decimal hours and total minutes consistently

Clock minutes are fractions of 60, not fractions of 100. Thirty minutes equals 0.50 hours, 15 minutes equals 0.25 and 45 minutes equals 0.75. For example, 7 hours 30 minutes is 7.50 decimal hours—not 7.30. The Total minutes result shows the same repeated-workday duration before conversion to decimal hours.

Use the pay estimate as a cross-check

The pay estimate multiplies the calculated hours by the hourly rate entered. It is a basic gross estimate and does not determine overtime, taxes, deductions, premiums or other payroll adjustments. If the workweek can include overtime, use the overtime or weekly timesheet calculator and verify the applicable rule.

Before you rely on the result

Check the start and end times, whether the shift crosses midnight, the unpaid-break minutes, the number of repeated workdays and the hourly rate. If the unpaid break is longer than the elapsed shift, the calculator returns zero hours for that day instead of a negative duration. Compare important payroll or billing totals with the source time record rather than using the calculator as the only record of hours worked.
