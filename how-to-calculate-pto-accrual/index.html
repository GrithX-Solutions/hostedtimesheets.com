PTO accrual guide

How to Calculate PTO Accrual

PTO can accrue by hours worked, by pay period, or as a fixed allocation. Use the method in your employer policy, then add carryover and subtract PTO used to estimate the balance.

Use the PTO Accrual Calculator

Quick answer

Calculate PTO accrual by applying the method in the policy: multiply eligible hours by an hourly accrual rate, add the fixed amount for each eligible pay period, or use the stated front-loaded allowance.

PTO accrual formulas

Per hour worked: PTO earned = hours worked × accrual rate.

Per pay period: PTO earned = pay periods × PTO hours per period.

Projected balance: current balance + carryover + PTO earned − PTO used.

Example by hours worked

If the accrual rate is 0.05 PTO hours per hour worked and you work 160 hours, you earn 8 PTO hours.

Example by pay period

If a policy grants 4 PTO hours each pay period, four completed pay periods add 16 PTO hours before usage or caps.

Carryover and balance caps

Carryover adds eligible unused time from an earlier period. A balance cap limits the maximum banked amount. The calculator applies only the values you enter; it does not decide which policy or legal rule applies.

Vacation and time-off accrual

The same arithmetic can be used for vacation or general time-off accrual when the policy uses an hourly, pay-period, or fixed method. Try the PTO Accrual Calculator as a vacation or time-off accrual calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate PTO accrual per hour?

Multiply hours worked by the PTO hours earned for each hour worked.

How do I calculate PTO accrual per pay period?

Multiply the number of completed pay periods by the PTO hours granted each period.

How do I calculate an hourly PTO accrual rate from an annual allowance?

Divide the annual PTO allowance by the annual eligible work hours defined by the policy. For example, 120 PTO hours ÷ 2,080 eligible hours is about 0.0577 PTO hour per eligible work hour.

PTO accrual method

Choose the accrual method that matches the policy

The arithmetic is simple only after you identify how the employer actually grants time off. A policy may accrue PTO for each eligible hour worked, add a fixed amount every pay period, or front-load a yearly balance.

Hourly accrual

Multiply eligible hours worked by the PTO earned per eligible hour. At a rate of 0.0577 PTO hours per work hour, 80 eligible hours produce about 4.62 PTO hours before policy-specific rounding.

Per-pay-period accrual

Use the fixed amount assigned to each eligible pay period. A 120-hour annual allowance spread over 26 biweekly periods is about 4.6154 hours per eligible period before rounding.

Front-loaded PTO

A front-loaded plan adds a stated balance at the start of a plan year or eligibility period. Do not convert it to hourly accrual unless the governing policy actually uses that method.

Derive an accrual rate from an annual allowance

If a policy states an annual allowance but payroll needs an accrual rate, divide annual PTO hours by the eligible work hours or pay periods defined by that policy. Per-hour rate = annual PTO hours ÷ annual eligible work hours. For 120 PTO hours and 2,080 eligible work hours, the mathematical rate is 0.057692. Forty eligible hours at that rate earn about 2.31 PTO hours before rounding.

Per-pay-period amount = annual PTO hours ÷ pay periods. With 120 hours across 24 semimonthly periods, each eligible period adds 5.00 hours. Across 26 biweekly periods, the amount is about 4.6154 hours. Biweekly and semimonthly schedules are different, so use the count in the actual policy.

Reconcile earned, used, carryover and caps separately

Keep each balance component visible. Start with the opening balance, add newly earned PTO and approved carryover, subtract PTO already used, then apply any balance cap if the policy has one. Projected balance = opening balance + earned PTO + carryover − PTO used.

A cap can limit the available balance or pause future accrual after a threshold, depending on the employer's rules. The calculator can model a numeric cap, but it cannot decide which cap, forfeiture, eligibility or carryover rule applies to a particular employee.

Worked example: biweekly accrual

Assume a policy provides 104 PTO hours per year across 26 eligible biweekly pay periods. The accrual is 104 ÷ 26 = 4 hours per period. If the employee starts with 12 hours, earns 4 hours and uses 6 hours, the projected balance is 10 hours: 12 + 4 − 6 = 10.

If eligibility begins partway through a year, a period is not accrual-eligible, the schedule changes, or a balance reaches its cap, recalculate from the exact policy inputs instead of multiplying the normal amount by every remaining period.

What to verify before relying on the result

Confirm the accrual method, eligible-hours definition, pay frequency, annual or per-period allowance, opening balance, carryover, PTO already taken, balance cap and rounding rule. These inputs affect the result more than the calculator itself.

PTO practices vary by employer and jurisdiction. Hosted Timesheets provides arithmetic and planning estimates; it does not determine legal eligibility, wage treatment, forfeiture or required leave. For payroll or employee records, compare the result with the governing policy and authoritative records.
