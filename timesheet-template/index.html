What this timesheet template includes

The template provides one row per day with fields for clock-in, clock-out, unpaid break minutes and total hours. Switch between weekly and biweekly layouts without creating an account.

Printable and spreadsheet-friendly

Use the print button for paper or PDF output, or download CSV to continue working in Excel, Google Sheets or another spreadsheet application.

Who can use it?

Employees, freelancers, contractors and small businesses can use the template for simple time records. For payroll or compliance use, verify that the format meets your employer and jurisdiction requirements.

How to use the free timesheet template

Choose weekly or biweekly, enter the employee and company names, then select the period start date. Add clock-in and clock-out times for each workday and record unpaid break minutes where needed. The template gives you a structured record that is easy to print, save as a PDF through your browser, or export as CSV.

If you need the hours calculated automatically instead of using a blank-style record, use the weekly timesheet calculator or biweekly timesheet calculator. For shifts with lunch deductions, use the timesheet calculator with lunch.

Weekly vs. biweekly timesheets

A weekly timesheet covers seven calendar days and is useful when hours are reviewed or paid every week. A biweekly timesheet covers fourteen days and is useful when two workweeks are submitted together. Use the format that matches your employer, client or payroll period.

Timesheet fields explained

Clock in records when work starts. Clock out records when work ends. Break minutes records unpaid break time that may need to be excluded from worked hours. Total hours is the resulting work-time figure for the day. Keep official schedules or time-clock records when accuracy matters.

A timesheet template is a reusable work-time record with fields for dates, clock-in and clock-out times, breaks and total hours.

Should I use a weekly or biweekly timesheet?

Use the schedule that matches your reporting or payroll period. Weekly covers seven days, while biweekly covers fourteen days.

Timesheet recordkeeping

A useful template makes the source of every total visible

The template is designed for a lightweight weekly or biweekly record. Keep dates, time entries, breaks and totals together so a reviewer can trace the calculation instead of seeing only a final number.

Complete identification and period fields first

Enter the employee or contractor name, the covered week or pay period, and any project or department information needed by the organization. A clear period prevents the same row of hours from being copied into the wrong payroll or invoice cycle.

Record time consistently from day to day

Use the same time format for all entries and record unpaid breaks separately when the template provides a break field. If a day has no work, leave it clearly blank or use the organization's approved notation rather than entering a value that could be mistaken for worked time.

Weekly versus biweekly templates

A weekly template covers one seven-day period. A biweekly template covers two consecutive weeks, but it is still useful to keep each week's subtotal visible. This makes it easier to review attendance, overtime assumptions and corrections without relying only on a combined two-week number.

Corrections should preserve an audit trail

If a punch or duration is corrected, update the underlying entry and note the reason when the workflow requires it. Avoid changing only the final total because that leaves reviewers unable to reproduce the result from the visible rows.

Print and CSV serve different workflows

A printable timesheet is convenient for signatures or a paper archive. CSV is better for spreadsheet review and importing structured rows. Before downloading either format, verify the date range, each daily entry, break deductions and totals.

This browser-based template is a convenience tool, not an approval system or payroll database. Keep the organization's authoritative record and retention requirements in mind when using an exported copy.
