Quick answer

Use percentage mode when a raise is quoted as a percent, flat mode when it is quoted as an annual dollar amount, or compare mode when you know the old and new salaries. The calculator keeps annual salary as the source and derives monthly, biweekly, weekly and hourly-equivalent values from it.

How to calculate a salary increase

For a percentage raise, multiply the current annual salary by the raise percentage, then add that annual increase to the current salary.

Annual increase = current salary × raise percentage ÷ 100

New salary = current salary + annual increase

Example: 5% raise on $60,000

A 5% raise on $60,000 adds $3,000 per year and produces a new annual salary of $63,000. The same $3,000 annual change equals $250 per month, about $115.38 per biweekly period and about $57.69 per week as arithmetic planning equivalents.

Flat annual raise

Flat mode makes a fixed annual raise a first-class input. Add the raise amount directly to current annual salary. If the current salary is greater than zero, the calculator also converts that flat raise into a percentage of the current salary.

New salary = current salary + flat annual raise

Raise percentage = flat annual raise ÷ current salary × 100

Example: $3,000 flat raise

A $3,000 annual raise on a $60,000 salary produces $63,000. Because $3,000 is 5% of $60,000, the percentage result is 5.00%. If the current salary is zero and the flat raise is positive, no finite percentage baseline exists, so the calculator shows N/A instead of inventing a percentage.

Compare an old salary with a new salary

Compare mode calculates both the dollar change and the percentage change from the old salary. The old or current salary is the denominator because it is the baseline. This mode also permits a lower new salary, so its result labels use change rather than increase.

Salary change = new salary − current salary

Percentage change = salary change ÷ current salary × 100

Example: salary decrease

Moving from $63,000 to $60,000 is a $3,000 decrease. The percentage change is −4.7619% because −$3,000 divided by the original $63,000 equals about −0.047619. The calculator keeps the exact annual values authoritative before formatting period equivalents.

Monthly, biweekly and weekly equivalents

The calculator divides annual current salary, annual new salary and annual change by fixed period counts: 12 months, 26 biweekly periods and 52 weeks. These figures are arithmetic planning equivalents. An employer may use a different payroll calendar, so they should not be read as a promise of a specific paycheck amount.

Hourly equivalent after a raise

Hourly equivalents use the hours per week and weeks per year that you enter. A 40-hour schedule across 52 weeks produces 2,080 annual hours. Current and new annual salaries are divided by the same annual-hour base, and the hourly change is the difference between those two equivalents.

Hourly equivalent = annual salary ÷ (hours per week × weeks per year)

Hours per week must be at least 0.25. Weeks per year must be a whole number from 1 through 53. If either schedule value is blank or invalid, annual and pay-period salary results still calculate; only hourly-equivalent outputs become unavailable.

Methodology and assumptions

Annual salary is the authoritative base for every mode. Percentage mode does not add pay-cut semantics: its percentage must be zero or greater. Flat mode also accepts zero or greater. Compare mode can produce positive, zero or negative change because it compares two annual salaries directly.

Currency changes formatting only. This calculator does not estimate taxes, take-home pay, overtime eligibility, benefits, bonuses, commissions, inflation or cost-of-living adjustments. If compensation includes a bonus, use the Salary Calculator With Bonus. To convert an annual salary to an hourly rate without raise arithmetic, use the Salary to Hourly Calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a 5% salary increase?

Multiply the current annual salary by 5%, then add that amount to the current salary. A 5% raise on $60,000 adds $3,000 and produces a new annual salary of $63,000.

How do I calculate a flat-dollar raise?

Add the flat annual raise amount to the current annual salary. A $3,000 flat raise on $60,000 produces $63,000. This calculator also shows the equivalent percentage when the current salary is greater than zero.

How do I calculate the percentage change from an old salary to a new salary?

Subtract the old salary from the new salary, divide the change by the old salary, then multiply by 100. The old salary is the baseline.

What if the current salary is zero?

If both current and new salary are zero, the percentage change is 0.00%. If the current salary is zero and the new salary or flat raise is greater than zero, no finite percentage baseline exists, so the calculator shows N/A.

How are monthly, biweekly and weekly equivalents calculated?

The calculator divides annual salary values by 12 for monthly, 26 for biweekly and 52 for weekly planning equivalents. These are arithmetic averages, not a prediction of a specific employer payroll calendar.

How is the hourly equivalent calculated?

The calculator divides annual salary by hours per week multiplied by weeks per year. Schedule inputs affect only hourly-equivalent outputs; annual and pay-period results still calculate when schedule inputs are invalid.

Does this calculator estimate taxes or take-home pay?

No. It calculates gross base-salary changes only. It does not estimate taxes, deductions, bonuses, commissions, benefits, inflation or take-home pay.
