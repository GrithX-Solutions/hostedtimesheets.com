Methodology: calculate timesheet pay

For each workday, the calculator finds the elapsed time between clock-in and clock-out, subtracts unpaid break minutes, and adds the result to the weekly total.

Daily paid time = clock-out − clock-in − unpaid break

Regular and overtime pay

The weekly total is compared with the overtime threshold you choose. Hours at or below the threshold use the regular hourly rate; hours above it use the regular rate multiplied by your overtime multiplier.

Gross pay = regular hours × rate + overtime hours × overtime rate

Example

Five shifts from 9:00 AM to 6:00 PM with a 30-minute unpaid break produce 42.5 paid hours. With a 40-hour threshold, 2.5 hours are overtime.

Overnight shifts

If clock-out is earlier than clock-in, the calculator treats the end time as the next calendar day.

Hours-to-pay workflow

Verify worked time before turning hours into pay

A pay estimate is only as reliable as the time entries and rate assumptions underneath it. Review each daily row first, then apply the hourly rate and overtime settings.

Net worked hours come before pay

For each day, subtract clock-in from clock-out, account for an overnight shift when needed, and subtract only the unpaid break time that belongs to that record. Add the verified daily amounts to obtain the weekly total before calculating regular or overtime pay.

Regular pay and overtime pay are separate calculations

If the entered threshold is 40 hours, the base rate is $22 and the overtime multiplier is 1.5, a 44-hour week is split into 40 regular hours and 4 overtime hours. The simple estimate is $880 regular pay plus $132 overtime pay, or $1,012 gross before other adjustments.

Gross pay is not take-home pay

The calculator does not subtract taxes, benefit deductions, garnishments or other withholdings. It also does not add bonuses, reimbursements, commissions, shift premiums or other earnings unless they are represented by the inputs. Treat the result as a basic gross-pay estimate.

One hourly rate cannot represent every payroll arrangement

Employees who work multiple jobs or rates, receive nondiscretionary bonuses, or have special premium calculations may need a more detailed payroll method. Do not combine unlike rates into one number unless that matches the payroll rule being used.

Rounding differences can affect the pay estimate

If the source timekeeping system rounds punches or daily totals, use the same approved hours here. A few minutes of difference across several days can change both the weekly total and the number of hours above an overtime threshold.

Before using the result, compare clock times, unpaid breaks, weekly hours, overtime setting, hourly rate and multiplier with the authoritative payroll record and applicable rules. For time-only reconciliation, use the Weekly Timesheet Calculator.
