Quick answer

This work hours calculator finds elapsed shift time, subtracts an unpaid break, and repeats the net daily time across the number of workdays you enter. It shows the same result as hours and minutes, decimal hours and total minutes.

Net worked minutes per day = elapsed shift minutes − unpaid break minutes

Total worked minutes = net worked minutes per day × workdays

Estimated gross pay = total worked minutes ÷ 60 × hourly rate

The calculator keeps minute-based arithmetic authoritative. It converts to decimal hours only for display and for the optional gross-pay multiplication.

How the work hours calculation works

Start with the elapsed time from the shift start to the shift end. If the end time is earlier than the start time, the calculator rolls the end into the following day. It then subtracts the unpaid break and prevents the net result from falling below zero.

Example: 9:00 AM to 5:30 PM with lunch

From 9:00 AM to 5:30 PM is 8 hours 30 minutes, or 510 minutes. Subtract a 30-minute unpaid break and the net workday is 480 minutes: 8 hours exactly, or 8.00 decimal hours. Repeat that same schedule for five workdays and the total is 2,400 minutes, 40 hours, or 40.00 decimal hours.

Example: overnight shift

A 10:00 PM to 6:00 AM shift crosses midnight and lasts 8 hours. With a 30-minute unpaid break, net worked time is 7 hours 30 minutes, which equals 7.50 decimal hours. The overnight example button loads this case directly into the calculator.

Hours and minutes versus decimal hours

Clock minutes are fractions of 60, not fractions of 100. Thirty minutes equals 0.50 hour, 15 minutes equals 0.25 hour and 45 minutes equals 0.75 hour. For example, 7h 30m and 7.50 decimal hours describe the same duration; 7.30 decimal hours would equal 7h 18m.

Use the Decimal Hours Calculator when you need a dedicated two-way time-to-decimal conversion. This page keeps both formats visible so you can cross-check work-hour totals without leaving the calculator.

Repeated schedules versus changing daily shifts

This calculator intentionally models one repeated shift pattern. Use it when the same start time, end time and break apply across several workdays. The Workdays field accepts whole numbers from 1 through 31.

If your punches change from day to day, use the Time Card Calculator. For split shifts or a detailed weekly schedule, use the Weekly Timesheet Calculator. Keeping those workflows separate avoids pretending that one repeated shift represents a variable schedule.

Break handling and edge cases

Enter only break minutes that you want excluded from worked time. The calculator subtracts that duration once after it finds the elapsed shift. If the break is longer than the elapsed shift, net worked time becomes zero instead of a negative number. If the start and end times are identical, the result is also zero rather than an assumed 24-hour shift.

The break field requires a whole number zero or greater. The calculator does not silently turn a blank, negative or fractional break into another value. Correct the highlighted field before using the result.

Optional gross-pay estimate

The hourly-rate field is optional. Leave it blank when you only need hours. If you enter a rate, the calculator multiplies the exact total worked minutes by the rate after dividing by 60. An explicit zero rate is valid and returns a zero pay estimate; a negative rate is rejected without hiding the valid time results.

This is gross arithmetic only. The calculator does not add overtime premiums, taxes, deductions, shift differentials or employer-specific adjustments. Use the Overtime Calculator when you need separate regular and overtime math.

Methodology and assumptions

The calculator uses the exact minute values represented by the time inputs and applies no time-clock rounding. It treats an earlier end time as next-day, subtracts the entered unpaid break, floors net daily time at zero, then multiplies that net time by whole repeated workdays.

Currency changes money formatting only. The calculator performs arithmetic from your inputs; it does not determine whether a break should be paid, whether overtime applies, or which payroll policy governs a record. Compare important payroll or billing totals with the source time record and the rules that actually apply.

Frequently asked questions

Is this a free work hours calculator?

Yes. It calculates one repeated shift pattern, unpaid break time, total work hours and an optional gross-pay estimate without signup.

How do I calculate hours worked with a lunch break?

Find the elapsed time between start and end, then subtract the unpaid lunch or break minutes. The calculator shows both hours-and-minutes and decimal-hour results.

Can it calculate overnight work hours?

Yes. If the end time is earlier than the start time, the calculator treats the end as the following day. Identical start and end times return zero rather than 24 hours.

How many hours is 9:00 AM to 5:30 PM with a 30-minute break?

The elapsed shift is 8 hours 30 minutes. Subtracting a 30-minute unpaid break leaves 8 hours worked, which is 8.00 decimal hours.

Why is 7 hours 30 minutes equal to 7.50 decimal hours?

Decimal hours use fractions of 60 minutes. Thirty minutes divided by 60 equals 0.50, so 7 hours 30 minutes equals 7.50 hours.

What happens if the unpaid break is longer than the shift?

The calculator floors net worked time at zero, so it never returns negative work hours. Check the source time record if that result was not intended.

Does this calculator calculate overtime?

No. The optional pay estimate uses all calculated hours at one hourly rate. Use the Overtime Calculator when you need separate regular and overtime arithmetic.

Work-hours math

Use elapsed time, break time and workdays as separate inputs

This calculator is designed for a repeated shift pattern. It calculates one net workday first, then multiplies that result by the number of workdays you enter.

Start with elapsed shift time

Convert the start and end times into one continuous duration. A 9:00 AM to 5:30 PM shift is 8 hours 30 minutes elapsed. If the end time is earlier than the start time, the calculator treats the shift as crossing midnight into the next day.

Subtract unpaid breaks after finding the elapsed shift

For a 9:00 AM–5:30 PM shift with a 30-minute unpaid lunch, net worked time is 8 hours. Use the break field only for time that should be excluded from worked time. If a separate timekeeping record already removes an off-the-clock meal period, avoid deducting it a second time.

Multiply only when the schedule really repeats

If the same 8-hour net shift occurs on five days, the weekly result is 40 hours. If start times, end times or breaks change from day to day, calculate each day separately instead of multiplying one assumed schedule. The weekly or time-card calculators are better suited to variable schedules.

Read decimal hours and total minutes consistently

Clock minutes are fractions of 60, not fractions of 100. Thirty minutes equals 0.50 hours, 15 minutes equals 0.25 and 45 minutes equals 0.75. For example, 7 hours 30 minutes is 7.50 decimal hours—not 7.30. The Total minutes result shows the same repeated-workday duration before conversion to decimal hours.

Use the pay estimate as a cross-check

The pay estimate multiplies the calculated hours by the hourly rate entered. It is a basic gross estimate and does not determine overtime, taxes, deductions, premiums or other payroll adjustments. If the workweek can include overtime, use the overtime or weekly timesheet calculator and verify the applicable rule.

Before you rely on the result

Check the start and end times, whether the shift crosses midnight, the unpaid-break minutes, the number of repeated workdays and the hourly rate. If the unpaid break is longer than the elapsed shift, the calculator returns zero hours for that day instead of a negative duration. Compare important payroll or billing totals with the source time record rather than using the calculator as the only record of hours worked.
