Time Card Hours With Lunch Break

Calculate the shift first, then subtract the unpaid lunch minutes. The break deduction should match the actual timekeeping or employer policy used for that record.

Calculate a weekly time card

Quick answer

Find the elapsed shift time first, then subtract the unpaid lunch. A 9:00 AM–5:30 PM shift with a 30-minute unpaid lunch equals 8.0 worked hours.

Worked time = clock out − clock in − unpaid lunch

Lunch break formula

Worked time = clock out − clock in − unpaid lunch

A lunch break changes the net worked time, not the clock-in or clock-out timestamps.

30-minute lunch example

A shift from 8:00 AM to 4:30 PM lasts 8 hours 30 minutes. Subtracting a 30-minute unpaid lunch leaves 8 hours worked.

8h 30m − 30m = 8h 00m

60-minute lunch example

A shift from 8:15 AM to 5:15 PM lasts 9 hours. If the full 60-minute lunch is unpaid, the time card records 8 hours worked.

9h 00m − 60m = 8h 00m

Different lunch lengths by day

Deduct each day separately. Do not assume every lunch was the same length when the source punches or approved records show otherwise.

Paid versus unpaid lunch

Only subtract the portion that should be excluded from worked time. Whether a meal period is paid can depend on employer policy and applicable rules, so the calculator leaves that decision to you.

Avoid double deductions

If the employee clocked out for lunch and back in, that gap may already be excluded from the recorded work periods. Do not subtract the same lunch again as a separate break.

Weekly lunch-break totals

After calculating each day, add the net daily hours. For a punch-by-punch week, use the Time Card Calculator; for a timesheet layout focused on lunch deductions, use the Timesheet Calculator With Lunch.

Frequently asked questions

How much do I subtract for a 30-minute lunch?

Subtract 30 minutes only when that 30-minute meal period should be unpaid.

Does a 1-hour lunch always reduce an 8-hour shift to 7 hours?

Only if the original elapsed shift is 8 hours and the full hour is unpaid. Calculate the actual start and end times first.

What if lunch already has separate clock-out and clock-in punches?

Add the worked segments on either side of lunch. Do not subtract the lunch again, because the off-the-clock gap already excludes that time.

Lunch-break time cards

Use the actual break record instead of a default deduction

A 30- or 60-minute lunch is common, but a time card should reflect the break that was actually recorded or approved. The correct deduction can vary by day.

Clocked-out lunch versus manual lunch deduction

If a time card has separate punches for the meal period, calculate the morning and afternoon work segments and add them. The lunch gap is then excluded automatically. If the employee stayed clocked in through an unpaid meal period, subtract the approved unpaid minutes once from the full elapsed shift.

Example with separate lunch punches

An employee works 8:00 AM–12:00 PM and 12:30 PM–4:30 PM. The first segment is 4 hours and the second is 4 hours, so net worked time is 8 hours. Subtracting another 30 minutes would double-count the lunch break and incorrectly reduce the day to 7.5 hours.

Lunch length can change from day to day

One day may have a 30-minute unpaid lunch and another may have 45 minutes. Calculate each day independently before adding the weekly total. A fixed daily deduction is convenient only when it matches the source time record and the policy being applied.

Paid and unpaid meal periods should not be mixed

The calculator subtracts whatever unpaid-break value you enter. It does not decide whether a meal period is legally or contractually paid. Use the employer's approved timekeeping rule and applicable requirements to decide what belongs in the unpaid-break field.

Split shifts and overnight work

For split shifts, total the worked segments and exclude the off-duty gaps. For an overnight shift, treat the clock-out as occurring on the following day when appropriate, then subtract the unpaid meal period. Example: 9:30 PM–6:00 AM is 8.5 elapsed hours; after a 30-minute unpaid break, net worked time is 8 hours.

Weekly lunch-break check

Before finalizing a weekly time card, compare the lunch deduction on each day with the source punches or approved record. Five 30-minute unpaid lunches equal 2 hours 30 minutes of weekly break time; five 60-minute lunches equal 5 hours. Large weekly differences often come from a break entered twice, omitted on one day or applied uniformly when the actual lengths varied.

Hosted Timesheets performs the time arithmetic from the values entered. It does not determine whether a break should be paid, whether a break was compliant, or what a payroll system must accept. Use the calculator as a reconciliation aid and keep the approved time record as the authoritative source.
