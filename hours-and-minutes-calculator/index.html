Quick answer

Total minutes = (hours × 60) + minutes

How to convert hours and minutes

Multiply the whole hours by 60, then add the remaining minutes.

Total minutes = (hours × 60) + minutes

To convert the same value into decimal hours, divide total minutes by 60.

Decimal hours = total minutes ÷ 60

Example

8 hours 30 minutes equals 510 total minutes and 8.50 decimal hours.

When this is useful

This conversion is useful when moving time between timesheets, payroll systems, invoices or spreadsheets that require either total minutes or decimal hours.

Duration is different from a time of day

This calculator converts a duration such as 8 hours 30 minutes. It does not subtract one clock time from another; use the Work Hours Calculator for start and end times.

Why total minutes can be useful

Total minutes are convenient when adding several durations or checking spreadsheet formulas. For example, 2h 15m is 135 minutes and 1h 50m is 110 minutes, so together they equal 245 minutes, or 4h 05m.

Frequently asked questions

Is 30 minutes equal to 0.30 hours?

No. Thirty minutes is half an hour, so it equals 0.50 decimal hours.

How many minutes are in 7.5 hours?

7.5 × 60 = 450 minutes.

Can this calculate pay too?

Yes. Enter an hourly rate and the calculator multiplies the decimal hours by that rate.

Duration conversion

Keep hours, minutes, total minutes and decimal hours connected

These are different representations of the same duration. Converting between them is useful for payroll, billing, project estimates and spreadsheet calculations.

Hours and minutes to total minutes

Multiply whole hours by 60 and add the remaining minutes. For 6 hours 35 minutes, 6 × 60 + 35 = 395 total minutes. Total minutes are useful when adding many short durations because they avoid carrying minutes manually after each addition.

Hours and minutes to decimal hours

Divide only the minute portion by 60 and add it to the whole hours. For 6h 30m, 30 ÷ 60 = 0.50, so the decimal value is 6.50. For 6h 20m, the decimal is about 6.3333. Clock minutes should never be copied directly after a decimal point unless the conversion happens to produce that value.

A duration is different from a clock time

“7:30” can describe a time of day, while “7h 30m” describes a duration. This calculator works with durations. To calculate the time between a start and end clock time, use the Work Hours Calculator instead.

Example for billing or payroll

A task lasting 2h 45m is 165 total minutes and 2.75 decimal hours. At a $40 hourly rate, that simple duration corresponds to $110 before any other billing or payroll adjustments. The rate calculation does not determine taxes, overtime, discounts or fixed-fee billing.

Reverse-check a decimal result

To convert a decimal back, keep the whole hours and multiply the fractional part by 60. For 4.25 hours, 0.25 × 60 = 15 minutes, so the duration is 4h 15m. A quick reverse conversion is a useful way to catch data-entry mistakes.
