How biweekly pay is calculated

Biweekly payroll means an employee is paid every two weeks. A standard year usually has 26 biweekly paychecks because 52 weeks divided by two equals 26.

Biweekly gross pay = annual salary ÷ pay periods per year

Example: $52,000 salary

A $52,000 annual salary divided across 26 pay periods equals $2,000 gross per biweekly paycheck. The weekly equivalent is $1,000 and the average monthly equivalent is about $4,333.33.

Hourly workers

For hourly employees, biweekly pay is based on two weeks of regular hours plus any recurring overtime entered. For example, $25 per hour at 40 regular hours per week produces $2,000 of regular gross pay over two weeks before overtime.

26 versus 27 pay periods

Some payroll calendars can contain 27 biweekly pay dates in a calendar year. For salaried employees, whether annual salary is divided across 26 or 27 checks depends on the employer's payroll method. Use the pay-period count that matches the actual payroll setup.

Biweekly is not semimonthly

Biweekly pay occurs every 14 days and normally creates 26 checks per year. Semimonthly pay usually occurs twice per month and creates 24 checks per year. The paycheck amount can therefore differ even when annual salary is the same.

Why taxes are excluded

Net pay depends on filing status, location, benefits and current tax rules. This calculator intentionally stays focused on gross-pay conversion so the arithmetic remains reusable for budgeting, offer comparisons and payroll planning.

Frequently asked questions

How much is $60,000 a year biweekly?

With 26 pay periods, $60,000 divided by 26 is about $2,307.69 gross per biweekly paycheck.

How many biweekly paychecks are in a year?

Most years have 26 biweekly paychecks. Some payroll calendars can have 27 depending on the pay-date schedule.

Does biweekly mean twice a month?

No. Biweekly means every two weeks; semimonthly means twice per month.

Does this calculator include taxes?

No. Results are gross pay before taxes, deductions and benefits.
