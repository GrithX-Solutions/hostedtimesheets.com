Quick answer

Use this free timesheet template to enter a weekly or biweekly record directly in your browser. Choose a period start, add clock-in and clock-out times, enter only break minutes you want excluded, and the template calculates daily and period totals before you print, save as PDF, or export CSV.

What this timesheet template includes

The template provides a Date, Day, Clock In, Clock Out, Break Minutes, and Hours field for each row. Weekly mode uses seven consecutive days. Biweekly mode uses fourteen days with separate Week 1 and Week 2 totals plus a combined total.

Printable and spreadsheet-friendly

Use Print Template for paper or your browser’s Save as PDF option. CSV export includes row dates, raw time entries, break minutes, exact net minutes, and decimal hours so you can review the same record in Excel, Google Sheets, or another spreadsheet application.

Who can use it?

Employees, freelancers, contractors and small businesses can use the template for simple time records. For payroll or compliance use, verify that the format meets your employer and jurisdiction requirements.

Methodology: how the free timesheet template works

  1. Choose Weekly or Biweekly and enter the employee, company, and period start when available.
  2. Enter each completed day’s clock-in and clock-out times.
  3. Enter only whole break minutes that you want excluded from worked time. The default is 0.
  4. Review daily hours and the weekly or biweekly totals before printing or exporting.

Net worked minutes = clock out − clock in − break minutes

If clock-out is earlier than clock-in, the template treats the shift as crossing midnight once. It adds exact valid-row minutes first and then derives period totals. It does not decide whether a break is paid, unpaid, required, or permitted.

Example: weekly record

Enter 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM with a 30-minute break for Monday through Friday. Each completed row equals 7h 30m, so the five-day weekly total is 37h 30m. Blank weekend rows do not add time.

Example: biweekly record

Use the same five-day 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM schedule with a 30-minute break in both weeks. Week 1 totals 37h 30m, Week 2 totals 37h 30m, and the combined biweekly total is 75h 00m.

Weekly vs. biweekly timesheets

A weekly timesheet covers seven consecutive calendar days. A biweekly timesheet covers fourteen consecutive days and keeps Week 1 and Week 2 subtotals visible before the combined total. Switching modes preserves entered rows in the current browser session, including Week 2 data when you switch back to Biweekly.

Timesheet fields explained

Date comes from the Period Start value and advances one calendar day per row. Clock In and Clock Out define the shift. Break Minutes subtract only the minutes you enter. Hours shows the exact minute-based daily duration in hours and minutes.

Invalid and overnight rows

A complete 10:00 PM to 6:00 AM shift with a 30-minute break equals 7h 30m. A row with only one punch, equal clock-in and clock-out times, a negative or fractional break, or a break longer than the shift is flagged and excluded from period totals until corrected.

Frequently asked questions

What is a timesheet template?

A timesheet template is a reusable work-time record with dates, clock-in and clock-out times, break minutes, daily hours, and period totals.

Does this timesheet template calculate hours automatically?

Yes. Complete clock-in and clock-out times produce minute-based daily hours, and valid rows roll into weekly or biweekly totals.

Should I use a weekly or biweekly timesheet?

Use the schedule that matches your reporting or payroll period. Weekly covers seven consecutive days, while biweekly covers fourteen days with separate week subtotals.

Can I download this timesheet template?

Yes. You can print it, use your browser to save it as PDF, or export a CSV that opens in Excel, Google Sheets, and similar spreadsheet tools.

Timesheet recordkeeping

A useful template makes the source of every total visible

The template is designed for a lightweight weekly or biweekly record. Keep dates, time entries, breaks and totals together so a reviewer can trace the calculation instead of seeing only a final number.

Complete identification and period fields first

Enter the employee or contractor name, the covered week or pay period, and any project or department information needed by the organization. A clear period prevents the same row of hours from being copied into the wrong payroll or invoice cycle.

Record time consistently from day to day

Use the browser time fields consistently and enter only break minutes that should be excluded from the duration. A blank row contributes nothing. The template does not turn a blank day into a worked day merely because its Break Minutes field displays 0.

Weekly versus biweekly templates

A weekly template covers one seven-day period. A biweekly template covers two consecutive weeks and shows each week’s subtotal before the combined total. These totals are arithmetic summaries only; the template does not determine overtime eligibility, pay rules, or payroll compliance.

Corrections should preserve an audit trail

If a punch or duration is corrected, update the underlying entry and note the reason when the workflow requires it. Avoid changing only the final total because that leaves reviewers unable to reproduce the result from the visible rows.

Print and CSV serve different workflows

Print output includes the employee, company, template type, period start, row dates, time entries, breaks, daily hours, and totals while hiding interactive controls. CSV keeps exact net minutes alongside decimal hours for reproducible spreadsheet review. Verify the source entries before using either copy.

This browser-based template is a convenience tool, not an approval system or payroll database. Keep the organization's authoritative record and retention requirements in mind when using an exported copy.
