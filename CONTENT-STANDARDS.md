# HostedTimesheets Content Quality Standard

Status: HARD RULE — mandatory for every new page and every material content update.

The site targets global English with U.S.-first search intent. Public-facing copy must read like natural, professional native English. No page is considered complete merely because it has the target keyword, a minimum word count, or a passing technical SEO score.

## 1. Semantic optimization

Every search-targeted page must:

- Satisfy one clear primary search intent and one primary keyword cluster.
- Use the primary topic naturally in the title, H1, opening answer, and relevant section headings where it improves clarity.
- Cover the entities, synonyms, calculations, assumptions, units, related questions, and adjacent concepts a user genuinely needs to complete the task.
- Use semantic variants naturally. Never force exact-match keywords or write to a keyword-density target.
- Avoid cannibalization. Improve the strongest existing page before creating a new URL for the same intent.
- Include contextual internal links to the most relevant parent, sibling, and next-step pages.
- Keep global-navigation links separate from contextual topical links; one does not replace the other.

## 2. Better than the current top competitors

Before publishing or materially rewriting a search-targeted page, review the current top organic results for the primary query and any visible AI Overview / People Also Ask patterns.

The page must provide substantial additional value for its actual intent, not simply more words.

A competitive review must check at least:

- Answer completeness
- Calculator/tool capability
- Formula and methodology clarity
- Worked examples
- Edge cases and assumptions
- Tables, steps, or other scan-friendly structures
- FAQ/question coverage
- Trust signals and authoritative references where relevant
- Privacy / no-signup explanation where relevant
- Freshness only where the underlying information changed
- Mobile usability and speed

The page must add at least two defensible information-gain elements compared with the majority of competing pages, such as a clearer method, a useful table, a reproducible example, a meaningful calculation mode, a well-defined limitation, a better privacy explanation, a stronger authoritative source, or a clearer answer to a recurring question.

Do not copy competitor wording, structure, claims, or examples.

## 3. Skimmability

For calculator and guide pages:

- Put the calculator/task first. Do not bury the tool below SEO copy.
- Add a concise Quick Answer near the start of the explanatory content.
- Use descriptive H2/H3 headings that communicate the answer even when scanned.
- Keep paragraphs focused on one idea.
- Preferred paragraph length: 20–70 words. Hard maximum: 90 words.
- Use numbered steps for procedures.
- Use bullets for options, assumptions, or grouped facts.
- Use tables for comparisons, reference values, examples, or conversions when a table is clearer than prose.
- Keep visible FAQs concise and useful. Do not create FAQ text only for schema.

## 4. Easy to read

Default writing targets:

- Native, neutral U.S. English suitable for a global audience.
- Average sentence length: 12–20 words.
- Rewrite sentences longer than 32 words unless the extra length is genuinely clearer.
- Prefer common words over jargon.
- Define technical payroll, PTO, time, or salary terminology at first use when a general reader may not know it.
- Remove filler, repetition, throat-clearing, and generic SEO introductions.
- Use concrete examples instead of abstract explanation where possible.

There is no fixed word-count target. Content should be as long as needed to fully satisfy the intent and no longer.

## 5. Grammar and punctuation

Hard requirement: zero known spelling, grammar, capitalization, or punctuation errors before publication.

Every page must receive:

1. Automated structural/content linting.
2. A native-English editorial review.
3. A final rendered-page proofread after deployment.

Do not publish awkward machine-translated phrasing, fragments presented as sentences, inconsistent capitalization, doubled punctuation, incorrect spacing around punctuation, or inconsistent terminology.

## 6. Active voice

Target at least 90% active-voice sentences in explanatory copy.

Use passive voice only when:

- The actor is genuinely unknown or irrelevant.
- A legal/compliance statement is more accurate in passive voice.
- Rewriting into active voice would sound unnatural or reduce clarity.

If a sentence can be made shorter and clearer by naming the actor, use active voice.

## 7. Calculator / tool page completeness

Unless the intent clearly makes an item unnecessary, a calculator page should include:

- One H1
- Clear task-oriented lead
- Quick Answer
- Formula or methodology
- Assumptions / limitations
- At least two reproducible worked examples
- At least three genuinely useful FAQs
- Related contextual internal links
- Visible update/review date only after a substantive update
- Matching structured data where appropriate
- Calculator output and examples that agree mathematically

## 8. Guide page completeness

A guide should include:

- Direct answer near the beginning
- Clear step-by-step method
- Formula where applicable
- At least two worked examples
- Common mistakes / edge cases where useful
- At least three useful FAQs when recurring questions exist
- Contextual links to the calculator that performs the task

## 9. Accuracy, trust, and sourcing

- Arithmetic and conversion claims must be reproducible.
- Legal, payroll-rule, tax, leave-policy, or jurisdiction-sensitive claims require an appropriate primary or authoritative source.
- State assumptions explicitly.
- Do not present a general calculator as legal, tax, payroll-processing, or HR advice.
- Do not fabricate authors, credentials, reviews, sources, statistics, dates, or product capabilities.
- Do not change a date solely to make a page appear fresh.

## 10. AEO / GEO / AI-answer readiness

Search-targeted pages should make their best factual answer easy to extract:

- Answer the core question directly before expanding.
- Use explicit formulas and named variables.
- Keep examples self-contained.
- Use visible FAQ content that matches FAQ schema exactly when FAQ schema is present.
- Identify Hosted Timesheets naturally where it adds trust or clarifies the tool.
- State browser-side/privacy behavior only when it is technically true.
- Cite authoritative sources for policy-sensitive claims.
- Avoid bloated, repetitive “AI SEO” prose.

## 11. Definition of done

A page is NOT done until all applicable checks pass:

- Search intent and semantic cluster reviewed
- Current competitor/SERP review completed
- Information gain documented
- Native-English editorial pass completed
- Skimmability/readability pass completed
- Active-voice pass completed
- Grammar/punctuation pass completed
- Calculator/formula/example consistency verified
- Internal links verified
- Structured data verified
- Mobile rendering verified
- Public HTTP 200 verified
- Sitemap/indexing update completed where needed

Technical SEO alone is not a content-quality pass.
