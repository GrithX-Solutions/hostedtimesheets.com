Quick answer

An overtime calculator separates regular hours from overtime hours and applies the rate you provide. In the default mode, enter total hours and the threshold you want to use. If payroll has already classified the hours, switch to Already-classified hours and enter regular, overtime and optional double-time hours directly.

Overtime rate = regular hourly rate × overtime multiplier

Overtime pay = overtime hours × overtime rate

Estimated gross pay = regular pay + overtime pay + double-time pay

Example: 45 hours at $25 per hour with a 40-hour threshold and a 1.5× multiplier produces 40 regular hours, 5 overtime hours, a $37.50 overtime rate and $1,187.50 estimated gross pay.

Two ways to calculate overtime pay

Total hours + threshold

Use this mode when you have one total-hours figure and want a simple arithmetic split at a threshold you choose. The calculator assigns hours up to that threshold to the regular bucket and hours above it to the overtime bucket. It does not decide whether that threshold is legally correct for a particular worker.

Regular hours = min(total hours, threshold)

Overtime hours = max(0, total hours − threshold)

Already-classified hours

Use this mode when an authoritative payroll process, employer record or applicable rule has already classified the hours. Enter regular hours, overtime hours and any double-time hours directly. This mode is useful when one threshold cannot describe the classification, while still keeping the calculator out of legal-rule selection.

Federal overtime is a rule boundary, not a calculator assumption

For covered nonexempt employees under the U.S. Fair Labor Standards Act, federal overtime is generally at least one and one-half times the regular rate after 40 hours in a workweek. Coverage, exemptions and special rules matter, and state law can differ. That is why this calculator lets you choose the threshold or enter already-classified hours instead of deciding eligibility for you.

The regular rate can also be more complex than a stated base wage. Certain payments may need to be included when an employer determines the FLSA regular rate. If your payroll includes bonuses, commissions, multiple rates or differentials, determine the correct regular rate first and then use the resulting rate only if a one-rate estimate is appropriate.

U.S. references: U.S. Department of Labor Fact Sheet #23 explains federal overtime requirements. Fact Sheet #56A explains the regular rate of pay.

Gross overtime pay is different from the qualified-overtime tax deduction

This page estimates gross pay before taxes and deductions. It does not calculate federal income tax, withholding or the qualified-overtime deduction. Current IRS guidance treats qualified overtime as a separate tax calculation with eligibility, reporting and limit rules; for ordinary FLSA time-and-a-half, the potentially qualified amount is generally the required premium above the regular rate rather than the entire overtime payment.

Tax reference: IRS Fact Sheet FS-2026-13 (August 2026). Verify current tax eligibility and reporting separately.

Methodology and assumptions

All calculations use the decimal-hour values you enter. The calculator does not reconstruct punches, round time-clock entries, classify employees, decide whether weekends or holidays receive a premium, determine daily overtime, calculate taxes or apply deductions. Use verified source hours and the rate inputs appropriate to your payroll situation.

When you use total-hours mode, the selected threshold produces the regular/overtime split. When you use classified-hours mode, your three hour buckets are authoritative inputs for the arithmetic. Zero hours and a zero hourly rate are valid values; missing, negative or invalid active inputs are rejected instead of silently becoming zero.

Frequently asked questions

What is time and a half?

Time and a half means an overtime rate equal to 1.5 times the regular hourly rate. For a $20 regular rate, a 1.5× overtime rate is $30 per overtime hour.

How do I calculate overtime pay?

Multiply overtime hours by the overtime hourly rate. This calculator can either split total hours using the threshold you enter or use regular and overtime hours that you have already classified.

Is overtime always after 40 hours in a week?

No single threshold applies to every worker or situation. For covered nonexempt employees under the U.S. FLSA, overtime is generally due after 40 hours in a workweek, but exemptions, state rules and other special rules can differ.

Can I enter overtime hours directly?

Yes. Choose the classified-hours mode when you already know which hours are regular, overtime or double time. The calculator uses your classification and does not decide which rule applies.

Can this calculator handle double-time pay?

Yes. In classified-hours mode, enter the double-time hours and the double-time multiplier you want to use. The calculator treats those values as user-provided arithmetic inputs rather than a legal determination.

Are weekends or holidays automatically overtime?

Not under the federal FLSA simply because the work occurs on a weekend or holiday. State law, an agreement or employer policy may provide different premium-pay rules.

Does this calculate the federal qualified overtime deduction?

No. This tool estimates gross regular, overtime and double-time pay. The federal qualified-overtime deduction is a separate tax calculation with its own eligibility, reporting and limit rules.
