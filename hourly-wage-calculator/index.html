Hourly wage to annual salary

For an hourly-to-annual conversion, multiply the hourly wage by regular hours worked each week and by the number of weeks worked in the year. A 40-hour schedule across 52 weeks uses 2,080 regular work hours.

Annual salary equivalent = hourly wage × hours per week × weeks per year

At that schedule, $20 per hour equals $41,600 per year, $25 per hour equals $52,000, and $30 per hour equals $62,400 before overtime, taxes and deductions.

Monthly and biweekly wage equivalents

After calculating annual gross pay, divide by 12 for an average monthly equivalent. For a regular biweekly equivalent, multiply weekly regular pay by 2. These are planning equivalents; an employer's actual payroll calendar can produce different paycheck amounts or counts.

Why weeks worked per year matters

Using 52 weeks assumes pay is earned across the full year. If you expect fewer paid workweeks, use the actual number. For example, $25 per hour at 40 hours per week for 50 weeks equals $50,000 instead of $52,000.

Methodology and assumptions

Regular pay uses the hourly rate, weekly regular hours and weeks worked that you enter. Optional overtime is calculated separately from the multiplier and overtime hours entered. Results are gross-pay arithmetic only and do not determine overtime eligibility, taxes or payroll deductions.

How to calculate hourly wage to annual salary

Multiply your hourly wage by regular hours worked each week, then multiply that weekly amount by the number of weeks you expect to work during the year.

Annual gross pay = hourly wage × weekly hours × weeks worked

Example: $25 per hour

At $25 per hour for 40 hours a week and 52 weeks a year, regular gross pay is $1,000 per week and $52,000 per year. The monthly equivalent is the annual amount divided by 12.

Hourly to annual with unpaid time off

If you do not expect to work all 52 weeks, lower the weeks-per-year field. For example, 50 paid workweeks gives a different annual result than a full 52-week schedule.

Optional overtime estimate

Overtime hours are calculated separately using the multiplier you enter. This is arithmetic only; the tool does not determine whether particular hours legally qualify for overtime.

Why this calculator avoids tax estimates

Taxes depend on filing details, location and current rules. Keeping this calculator focused on gross wage conversion makes the result reusable for budgeting, job comparisons and payroll planning.
