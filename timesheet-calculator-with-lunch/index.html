Methodology and assumptions

Each row is calculated independently from the times entered. Unpaid lunch minutes are subtracted from elapsed shift time, overnight shifts roll into the following day, and weekly totals are the sum of the net daily results. Overtime and pay outputs are arithmetic estimates based on the threshold, multiplier and rate entered; they do not determine which hours legally qualify for overtime.

The calculator applies rounding to each day's net minutes after it subtracts the unpaid break and before it adds weekly totals. Identical clock-in and clock-out times return 0 rather than a 24-hour shift. If an unpaid break is longer than the elapsed shift, the calculator floors the daily result at 0.

U.S. reference: the U.S. Department of Labor Fact Sheet #22 distinguishes short rest periods from bona fide meal periods for hours-worked purposes. Use the rules and approved records that apply to the worker.

How to calculate timesheet hours with a lunch break

For each workday, subtract your clock-in time from your clock-out time and then subtract any unpaid lunch or break minutes. Add the daily totals to get weekly hours.

Basic formula

Worked time = Clock out − Clock in − unpaid break

If a shift ends after midnight, this calculator treats it as an overnight shift and continues into the next day.

Example

If you clock in at 9:00 AM, clock out at 5:30 PM, and take a 30-minute unpaid lunch, the calculator returns 8.00 worked hours.

Overtime and pay

You can choose an overtime threshold and multiplier. These settings are configurable because overtime rules differ by employer and jurisdiction. The calculator does not provide legal or payroll advice.

Frequently asked questions

Does the calculator subtract lunch automatically?

Yes. Enter the unpaid break in minutes for each day. A paid lunch should not be entered as an unpaid break.

Can it calculate overnight shifts?

Yes. If clock-out is earlier than clock-in, the calculator assumes the shift ended the following day.

What does decimal hours mean?

Decimal hours express minutes as a fraction of an hour. For example, 8 hours 30 minutes equals 8.50 decimal hours.

How do I calculate weekly work hours with lunch breaks?

For each day, subtract clock-in time from clock-out time, subtract the unpaid lunch, then add the net daily totals. Five 8-hour net days equal 40 weekly hours.

Can I use a different lunch length on each day?

Yes. Each timesheet row has its own unpaid-break value, so you can enter 30 minutes on one day, 60 minutes on another, or zero when there is no unpaid lunch.

Can I use this as a time card calculator with a lunch break?

Yes. It is designed for one clock-in, one clock-out and one unpaid break per day. For multiple split shifts on the same day, use the Weekly Timesheet Calculator instead.

Lunch-break timesheet

Make the lunch deduction match the actual record

This calculator is optimized for a weekly timesheet where each day has one clock-in, one clock-out and an unpaid lunch or break. The most important input is the break duration actually recorded or approved for that day.

Thirty-minute and sixty-minute lunches change the net day differently

A 9:00 AM–5:30 PM shift is 8.5 elapsed hours. Subtracting a 30-minute unpaid lunch produces 8.0 worked hours. Subtracting a 60-minute unpaid lunch produces 7.5. Enter the appropriate value day by day instead of assuming every lunch is identical.

Do not deduct a meal period twice

If the employee's source time record already contains a clock-out for lunch and a later clock-in, the lunch gap has already been removed from worked time. This calculator uses a single in/out pair plus a break field, so translate the source record carefully before entering it.

Overnight shifts still need the break deduction

A shift from 10:00 PM to 6:30 AM spans 8.5 hours. After a 30-minute unpaid meal period, net worked time is 8 hours. The calculator treats an earlier clock-out time as the following day, then applies the break value you entered.

Weekly lunch totals are a useful error check

Five 30-minute unpaid lunches remove 2.5 hours from the week's elapsed shift time. Five 60-minute lunches remove 5 hours. If the weekly result looks unexpectedly low or high, compare the accumulated break minutes with the source records before changing clock times.

Pay and overtime are estimates layered on top of time

First verify the worked-time total. Then review the overtime threshold, multiplier, hourly rate and currency. The pay estimate does not account for taxes, deductions, bonuses, multiple rates, shift premiums or jurisdiction-specific requirements.
