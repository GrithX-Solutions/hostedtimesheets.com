Quick answer

If the bonus is a percentage of salary, calculate the bonus first:

Annual bonus = base salary × bonus percentage ÷ 100

Example: a $60,000 salary with a 10% annual bonus adds $6,000, producing $66,000 in total gross annual compensation.

How to calculate a bonus as a percentage of salary

Multiply base annual salary by the bonus percentage, then divide by 100. For example, an 8% bonus on a $70,000 salary is $5,600. Adding that bonus to base salary gives $75,600 in gross annual compensation.

Find the bonus percentage from target compensation

If you know the target total compensation and base salary, subtract base salary to find the bonus amount. Then divide that bonus by base salary and multiply by 100.

Required bonus % = (target compensation − base salary) ÷ base salary × 100

This reverse formula requires a positive base salary. If base salary is zero, a bonus percentage relative to salary is undefined.

Example: an $80,000 base salary with a $90,000 target total compensation requires a $10,000 bonus. $10,000 ÷ $80,000 × 100 = 12.5%.

Fixed bonus versus percentage bonus

A fixed bonus is entered directly as a dollar amount. A percentage bonus changes with base salary because it is calculated from salary. This calculator supports both methods so you can model an offer, target bonus or recurring annual bonus without converting the number manually.

Monthly, biweekly and weekly equivalents

Total annual compensation is divided by 12 for an average monthly equivalent, by 26 for a biweekly equivalent and by 52 for a weekly equivalent. These are planning equivalents, not predictions of the exact paycheck on which a bonus will be paid.

Hourly equivalent including bonus

The calculator converts base salary and total compensation to hourly equivalents using only the Hours/week and Workweeks/year inputs. Those two schedule inputs do not change the monthly, biweekly or weekly equivalents above. With 40 hours per week and 52 workweeks per year, the annual-hours assumption is 2,080 hours.

Methodology and assumptions

Percentage mode applies the entered bonus percentage to base annual salary. Fixed mode adds the entered annual bonus amount directly. Total compensation is base salary plus annual bonus. Pay-period and hourly figures are arithmetic equivalents only. The calculator clamps Workweeks/year to the visible 1–53 range and Hours/week to a minimum of 0.25 for hourly-equivalent math.

If base salary is zero, the calculator shows the bonus percentage of base salary as N/A instead of displaying a misleading 0%. Taxes, withholding, bonus eligibility, vesting, commissions, equity, benefits and employer-specific payout schedules are excluded.

Gross salary + bonus, not a bonus-tax calculator

Current search results for salary-with-bonus queries often mix gross compensation tools with bonus-tax and take-home-pay calculators. This page intentionally calculates gross salary, gross bonus and arithmetic equivalents only; it does not estimate bonus withholding or take-home pay.

Salary with bonus vs. salary increase

A bonus can be variable or one-time and may not permanently change base salary. A salary increase changes recurring base pay. To model a permanent raise instead, use the Salary Increase Calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What is a 10% bonus on $60,000?

A 10% bonus on $60,000 is $6,000, producing $66,000 in total gross annual compensation.

How do I calculate bonus percentage from a dollar amount?

Divide the bonus amount by base salary and multiply by 100. For example, a $5,000 bonus on a $50,000 salary equals 10%.

Does this calculator include bonus taxes?

No. It calculates gross salary and bonus amounts before taxes, withholding, deductions and benefits.

Is a bonus the same as a salary raise?

No. A salary raise changes recurring base salary. A bonus is additional compensation and may be variable or paid separately.

Can I enter a fixed bonus instead of a percentage?

Yes. Select fixed annual bonus and enter the bonus amount directly.

What bonus percentage reaches a target total compensation?

Subtract base salary from target total compensation, divide the difference by base salary, and multiply by 100. For example, $90,000 target compensation on an $80,000 salary requires a 12.5% bonus.

What happens if base salary is zero?

The calculator can still add a fixed bonus amount, but a bonus percentage relative to a zero base salary is undefined, so the percentage-of-salary result shows N/A.
