Quick answer

Billable utilization is billable hours divided by total worked hours, multiplied by 100. Billable value is billable hours multiplied by the billing rate. This calculator uses total worked hours as the denominator, so compare its utilization percentage only with reports that use the same denominator.

How to calculate billable utilization

Billable utilization measures what share of worked time is chargeable to clients.

Billable utilization = billable hours ÷ total worked hours × 100

Example

If you work 40 hours and 30 are billable, your utilization is 75%. The remaining 10 hours are non-billable.

Estimated billable value

Multiply billable hours by your billable hourly rate. For 30 billable hours at $100 per hour, estimated billable value is $3,000. The rate is optional, so you can use the calculator only for utilization when no billing rate applies.

Effective value per worked hour

Billable rate applies only to billable time. Dividing billable value by all worked hours spreads that value across the full period. At 30 billable hours, 40 worked hours and a $100 billing rate, the effective value is $75 per worked hour.

Effective value per worked hour = billable value ÷ total worked hours

What counts as non-billable time?

Common examples include internal meetings, training, administration, business development, uncharged project work and other time that is not invoiced to a client.

Utilization targets

There is no universal target that fits every role or business. Managers, consultants, agencies and freelancers may use different targets depending on delivery responsibilities, sales work, management duties and pricing model.

When you enter a target, the calculator shows the target billable hours for the same worked-hours denominator, your signed hours difference, the billable value at that target when a rate is available, and the signed value difference. A negative difference means the current period is below the entered target; a positive difference means it is above.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between billable hours and worked hours?

Worked hours include all time spent working. Billable hours are the subset that can be charged to a client or project.

Can utilization exceed 100% in this calculator?

No. Because billable hours are treated as a subset of the same period’s worked hours, entering billable hours above total worked hours is invalid rather than silently capped.

Is a billing rate required?

No. Leave the rate blank to calculate utilization, non-billable hours and target-hour differences without a money estimate.

What does effective value per worked hour mean?

It is estimated billable value divided by total worked hours. It spreads the value of billable time across all worked time and is not the same as pay, profit or a collected hourly rate.

How is the target value difference calculated?

The calculator finds target billable hours from total worked hours and the target utilization, multiplies those hours by the entered billing rate, then compares that target value with current billable value.

What happens when total worked hours are zero?

Billable utilization is shown as N/A because zero billable hours divided by zero worked hours is undefined. Other arithmetic that remains defined, such as zero billable value, can still be shown.

Does changing currency convert my billing rate?

No. Currency selection changes money formatting only. It does not apply an exchange rate or convert the billing rate you entered.

Does this calculate actual invoiced or collected revenue?

No. It estimates billable value from hours and rate. Invoices, recognized revenue and collections can differ because of discounts, write-offs, fixed-fee work, taxes, expenses, realization and payment status.

Utilization & billing

Billable hours measure client work, not total work time

The calculator separates billable and non-billable time so utilization and estimated revenue can be reviewed without confusing them with payroll hours.

Billable utilization uses a defined capacity base

A common arithmetic form is billable utilization = billable hours ÷ available or worked hours × 100. The denominator matters. A team may measure against total worked hours, scheduled capacity or another internal target, so compare percentages only when the same definition is used.

Non-billable work can still be necessary work

Administration, internal meetings, training, business development and other activities may be non-billable even though they are legitimate working time. Do not subtract non-billable hours from payroll records simply because they do not generate client revenue.

Example utilization calculation

If a consultant records 30 billable hours and 10 non-billable hours in a 40-hour workweek, billable utilization is 30 ÷ 40 = 75%. If the internal target is 80%, the target would require 32 billable hours on the same 40-hour denominator.

Billable value and effective value answer different questions

At $150 per billable hour, 30 billable hours produce $4,500 of estimated billable value. Across a 40-hour worked period, that equals $112.50 of billable value per worked hour. The first figure prices client-chargeable time; the second shows how non-billable time changes the value produced across the full period.

Neither number proves invoice value, recognized revenue, profit or cash collected. Write-offs, fixed-fee arrangements, discounts, realization, taxes, expenses and payment status can change the financial result.

Target gaps are scenario arithmetic, not performance rules

At 40 total worked hours and an 80% target, target billable hours equal 32. If current billable hours are 30, the period is 2 billable hours below that entered target. At a $150 billing rate, the simple target-value difference is $300. The calculator does not recommend 80% or any other target.

Use consistent periods and definitions

When comparing people, projects or months, keep the time period, billable definition, denominator and target consistent. This page uses total worked hours as its denominator. Other systems may use scheduled, available or capacity hours, so percentages with different denominators are not directly comparable.

For payroll calculations use a Work Hours Calculator or Timesheet Pay Calculator. For hour-and-minute conversion use the Hours & Minutes Calculator or Decimal Hours Calculator. For client billing, compare this arithmetic with the approved project time and billing system.
