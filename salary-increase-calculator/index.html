How to calculate a salary increase percentage

If you know the current salary and the new salary, subtract the current salary from the new salary. Divide that difference by the current salary, then multiply by 100.

Use the old salary as the baseline. Dividing by the new salary answers a different question and understates the raise percentage. For example, a move from $60,000 to $66,000 is a 10% increase because the $6,000 gain is measured against the original $60,000.

Increase percentage = (new salary − current salary) ÷ current salary × 100

For example, moving from $60,000 to $66,000 is a $6,000 increase. Dividing $6,000 by $60,000 gives 0.10, so the salary increase is 10%.

Monthly, biweekly and weekly raise impact

The annual raise amount can be converted into an average monthly increase by dividing by 12, a biweekly increase by dividing by 26, and a weekly increase by dividing by 52. These are gross-pay equivalents and may not match an employer's exact paycheck schedule if payroll uses a different number of periods.

For a $60,000 salary with a 5% raise, the annual increase is $3,000. That equals an average increase of $250 per month, about $115.38 per biweekly pay period, or about $57.69 per week before taxes and deductions.

Hourly equivalent after a raise

For salary comparisons, the calculator converts both the current and new annual salary into hourly equivalents using the weekly hours and workweeks you enter. A 40-hour schedule across 52 weeks uses 2,080 annual work hours.

Hourly equivalent = annual salary ÷ (hours per week × weeks per year)

Flat-dollar raise

If your raise is quoted as a fixed annual amount instead of a percentage, add that amount to the current salary, then enter the current and new salaries in compare mode. For example, a $3,000 raise on $60,000 produces $63,000; compare mode returns a $3,000 annual increase and a 5% change.

New salary = current salary + fixed annual raise

Pay increase percentage calculator

When you know the old salary and the new salary, this tool also works as a pay increase percentage calculator. Subtract the old salary from the new salary, divide that increase by the old salary, then multiply by 100.

Pay increase percentage = (new salary − old salary) ÷ old salary × 100

Example: moving from $60,000 to $63,000 is a $3,000 increase. $3,000 ÷ $60,000 × 100 = 5%.

How much of a raise did I get?

Use compare-salaries mode when you only know the before-and-after amounts. The calculator returns both the annual dollar change and the percentage change, then shows monthly, biweekly, weekly and hourly equivalents. If the new salary is lower, the dollar and percentage changes are negative.

Methodology and assumptions

Percentage mode applies the entered raise percentage to current annual gross salary. Compare mode calculates the difference and percentage change between current and new annual salary. If current salary is zero and new salary is greater than zero, no finite percentage-change baseline exists, so the calculator shows N/A for the percentage while still showing the dollar change.

Monthly, biweekly and weekly changes use fixed annual equivalents of 12, 26 and 52 periods. Hourly equivalents use the entered hours per week and weeks per year. Taxes, bonuses, commissions, benefits, inflation and cost-of-living changes are not included.

Salary increase vs. bonus

A salary increase changes recurring base pay. A one-time bonus does not permanently change base salary, so it should normally be evaluated separately. If you need to convert your new annual salary back to an hourly rate, use the Salary to Hourly Calculator.
