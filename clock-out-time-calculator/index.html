Clock Out Time Calculator

Enter when you clocked in, how much paid time you need to work and any unpaid break. The calculator shows the exact time you can clock out, including overnight finishes.

What time should I clock out?

Use paid work time plus any unpaid break that extends your shift.

Quick answer

To find your clock-out time, add the paid work duration you need and any unpaid break to your clock-in time.

Clock-out time = clock-in time + paid work target + unpaid break

If you start at 8:30 AM, need 8 paid hours and take a 30-minute unpaid lunch, the total span is 8 hours 30 minutes and you clock out at 5:00 PM.

How to calculate clock-out time

Convert the paid work target and unpaid break to minutes, add them to the clock-in time, then wrap the result past midnight when necessary.

Total span minutes = paid work minutes + unpaid break minutes

Clock-out = clock-in + total span minutes

8-hour shift with a 30-minute lunch

Clock in at 8:30 AM. Add 8 paid hours and a 30-minute unpaid lunch. The calculated clock-out time is 5:00 PM (17:00), with 8 hours of paid work across an 8-hour-30-minute on-site span.

7-hour-30-minute shift with no unpaid break

Clock in at 9:00 AM and enter 7 paid hours 30 minutes with a 0-minute unpaid break. The result is 4:30 PM (16:30).

Overnight shift

Clock in at 10:00 PM and enter 8 paid hours with no unpaid break. The calculator returns 6:00 AM and marks it as the next day.

Paid breaks and unpaid breaks

Add only break time that extends the clock-out time without counting toward the paid-work target. If a break is paid and already counts toward the required paid hours, enter 0 unpaid break minutes.

The calculator does not decide whether a break should be paid, unpaid or required. Use the schedule, approved time record or policy that applies to you.

Same-day and next-day results

The tool uses local clock arithmetic and automatically carries a result past midnight. A start time of 10:00 PM plus 8 hours produces 6:00 AM the next day. Longer inputs can show a result one or more days later.

Daylight-saving transitions can make actual elapsed time differ from simple local-clock arithmetic. For date-aware elapsed-time calculations, use the Elapsed Time Calculator.

Why this is different from a time card calculator

This page answers a planning question: “What time should I clock out?” If you already know your clock-in and clock-out times and want total worked hours, breaks, overtime or pay, use the Time Card Calculator or Work Hours Calculator.

Methodology and limits

The calculator clamps paid hours to 0–72, paid minutes to 0–59 and unpaid break minutes to 0–1,440. It performs arithmetic from the values you enter. It does not determine overtime eligibility, required shift length, break entitlement, payroll rounding or whether time is compensable.

Frequently asked questions

What time should I clock out for an 8-hour shift?

Add 8 paid hours plus any unpaid break to your clock-in time. If you start at 8:30 AM and take a 30-minute unpaid lunch, you clock out at 5:00 PM.

Does an unpaid lunch change my clock-out time?

Yes. Add unpaid break minutes because they extend the time between clock-in and clock-out without adding paid work time. Enter 0 for a paid break.

Can the clock-out time be after midnight?

Yes. The calculator carries the result into the next day automatically and labels a next-day or later finish.

Can I enter a 7 hour 30 minute shift?

Yes. Enter 7 paid hours and 30 paid minutes, then add any unpaid break separately.

Does this calculator decide how long my break should be?

No. Enter the work target and unpaid break that match your schedule or approved record. The calculator performs time arithmetic only.

Clock-out planning

Keep paid work and unpaid break time separate

A correct leave time depends on whether your target describes paid work or the full time between clock-in and clock-out.

Eight paid hours is not always an eight-hour span

An 8:30 AM to 4:30 PM span is eight clock hours. If that span contains a 30-minute unpaid lunch, it includes only 7 hours 30 minutes of paid work. To reach 8 paid hours with that lunch, stay until 5:00 PM.

Use minutes for partial-hour targets

Do not treat 30 minutes as 0.30 of an hour. A 7-hour-30-minute paid target is 450 minutes. Starting at 9:00 AM with no unpaid break produces a 4:30 PM clock-out time.

Overnight arithmetic stays continuous

When the total extends past midnight, the calculator wraps the displayed clock time and keeps a day marker. Starting at 10:00 PM for 8 paid hours ends at 6:00 AM the next day.

Use the right tool for the question

Use this calculator when the desired output is your planned clock-out time. Use Work Hours when you know both times and need the duration, or the Weekly Timesheet Calculator when you need a full week of entries and totals.
