Quick answer

Calculate each shift, subtract unpaid breaks, then add all daily worked time for the week. Overtime can be separated after the weekly total using the threshold you enter.

How to calculate weekly timesheet hours

For each day, the calculator adds the duration of Shift 1 and Shift 2, subtracts any unpaid break minutes, then combines the daily totals for the week.

Weekly formula

Weekly hours = sum of daily worked minutes ÷ 60

If your total exceeds the overtime threshold you select, the excess is shown separately as overtime.

Split shift example

If you work 9:00 AM–12:30 PM and 1:00 PM–5:30 PM, the two shifts total 8.00 hours. This is useful when your unpaid meal period is represented as a real clock-out and clock-in rather than a break deduction.

Overnight shifts

If an end time is earlier than its start time, that shift is treated as continuing past midnight into the next day.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use both split shifts and unpaid breaks?

Yes. The calculator first adds both shifts and then subtracts the unpaid break minutes you enter.

Does it calculate overtime?

Yes. Choose the weekly overtime threshold and multiplier that apply to your use case.

Can I export the weekly time card?

Yes. You can print it or download the daily rows as CSV.

Weekly time card

Build the weekly total from verified daily entries

The weekly calculator supports one or two work periods per day, unpaid breaks, overnight work, overtime settings and an estimated pay total. Treat each daily row as a separate record before relying on the weekly sum.

Use Shift 2 only for a second worked period

A split shift should be calculated as two separate in/out pairs. For example, 8:00 AM–12:00 PM plus 2:00 PM–6:00 PM equals 8 hours worked. The two-hour gap is not work time unless the underlying record says otherwise.

Enter unpaid breaks once

If a lunch or break is not already excluded by separate clock-out and clock-in punches, enter the unpaid minutes in the break field. If the gap is already represented by the punch pairs, do not subtract it again. Double deductions are a common cause of understated weekly totals.

Check overnight shifts by date

When an end time is earlier than its start time, the calculator treats the end as occurring the next day. A 10:30 PM–6:30 AM shift is 8 elapsed hours before any unpaid break. For payroll records, keeping the actual dates with the punches reduces ambiguity around midnight.

Weekly overtime is a classification step, not just addition

The calculator can split hours above the threshold you enter and apply an overtime multiplier for an estimate. It does not decide which threshold or multiplier is legally required. Daily overtime, multiple pay rates, shift differentials and other special rules may require a different payroll workflow.

Example weekly review

If Monday through Thursday each produce 8 hours and Friday produces 9.5 hours, the weekly total is 41.5 hours. With a 40-hour threshold entered, the calculator will display 40 regular hours and 1.5 overtime hours. Verify that classification against the rules that apply before using it for payroll.

Print and CSV are output formats, not source records

The print view and CSV download make it easier to save or share the calculated entries. Before exporting, compare the clock times, split shifts, break deductions, overtime setting and hourly rate with the approved source record. Keep corrections traceable rather than changing totals without documenting the underlying punch.
