Decimal payroll hours = hours + (minutes ÷ 60)

Methodology: convert payroll hours

Payroll systems often use decimal hours rather than hours-and-minutes notation. Divide each day's minutes by 60, then add the result to the whole hours.

Decimal payroll hours = hours + (minutes ÷ 60)

Example

8 hours 15 minutes equals 8.25 payroll hours. 7 hours 45 minutes equals 7.75 payroll hours.

Payroll-ready time

Convert verified durations into the decimal format payroll expects

Payroll systems often store hours as decimal numbers even when employees record hours and minutes. Convert the time only after you verify worked time and unpaid breaks.

Minutes are fractions of 60

Convert the minute portion by dividing by 60. Fifteen minutes is 0.25 hour, 30 minutes is 0.50, and 45 minutes is 0.75. A duration of 8 hours 20 minutes equals 8 + 20 ÷ 60 = 8.3333… hours, not 8.20.

Add daily payroll hours with consistent precision

When daily durations include repeating decimals, keep enough precision during the intermediate steps. If the receiving payroll system requires two decimals, follow its documented rounding point. Rounding every day early can produce a slightly different weekly total than summing precise values first.

Worked example

Suppose three days are 7h 30m, 8h 15m and 8h 45m. Their decimal values are 7.50, 8.25 and 8.75, for a total of 24.50 payroll hours. If the source duration still includes an unpaid break, resolve that before conversion rather than subtracting a decimal guess afterward.

Overtime classification is a separate payroll step

A decimal-hours total shows how much time the record contains; it does not by itself decide which hours are overtime. Use the overtime threshold and policy that actually apply, and avoid assuming that every payroll system classifies hours in the same way. For a one-rate payroll estimate, use the Payroll and Overtime Calculator to separate regular pay from overtime pay.

Pay estimate and payroll import checks

The optional pay estimate is a simple hours-times-rate calculation. Before importing or copying values into payroll, compare the weekly total, decimal precision, overtime treatment and rate with the payroll system's documented requirements and approved time record.
