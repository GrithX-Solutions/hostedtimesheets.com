How the biweekly timesheet works

A biweekly timesheet covers fourteen calendar days. Enter each shift, subtract unpaid breaks, and the calculator totals each week separately before combining the two-week period.

Why weekly overtime is separated

Many payroll workflows evaluate overtime by workweek rather than by the combined two-week total. This tool therefore applies your chosen threshold to Week 1 and Week 2 separately.

Example

If Week 1 totals 42 hours and Week 2 totals 38 hours with a 40-hour threshold, the result is 80 total hours with 2 overtime hours.

Rounding and edge cases

For each day, the calculator finds the elapsed shift, treats an earlier clock-out as the next day, subtracts the unpaid break, floors a negative result at zero, and then rounds that day's net minutes to the nearest selected increment. Identical clock-in and clock-out times return zero rather than a 24-hour shift.

Estimated gross pay = regular hours × hourly rate + overtime hours × hourly rate × overtime multiplier

U.S. federal reference: U.S. Department of Labor Fact Sheet #23 explains that FLSA overtime applies on a workweek basis for covered, nonexempt employees. The DOL overtime guidance states that averaging hours over two or more weeks is not permitted under the FLSA. This calculator does not determine coverage, exemptions, state rules or the threshold that applies to a worker.

FAQ

Can I use a different lunch break on each day?

Yes. Each of the 14 daily rows has its own unpaid-break field, so lunch deductions can vary by day.

How do I calculate a two-week time card with lunch breaks?

For each day, subtract the unpaid break from the clock-in to clock-out duration. Add the first seven daily totals for Week 1, repeat for Week 2, then combine both weekly totals.

Does the calculator use an 80-hour overtime threshold for two weeks?

No. It applies the weekly overtime threshold you enter separately to Week 1 and Week 2 instead of treating the combined two-week total as one 80-hour threshold.

Can I print the result?

Yes. Use the print button for a clean printable view.

Two-week timekeeping

What a biweekly calculator should keep separate

A biweekly pay period contains two seven-day weeks, but weekly overtime rules and payroll totals are not automatically the same calculation. This tool shows each week separately so the inputs can be checked before the two-week total is used.

Calculate each day before combining the two weeks

For each workday, calculate clock-out minus clock-in and subtract the unpaid break entered for that day. If a shift crosses midnight, the end time is treated as the following day. Add the seven daily results for Week 1, repeat for Week 2, and then combine both weekly totals for the pay-period total.

Why the calculator keeps each week separate

Two weeks containing 40 hours each total 80 hours, but a different pattern such as 46 hours in Week 1 and 34 hours in Week 2 still totals 80. A simple 80-hour pay-period total hides the weekly distribution. The calculator therefore applies the threshold you enter to each seven-day week separately instead of averaging the two weeks together.

Example: same biweekly total, different weekly pattern

Employee A records 40 hours in Week 1 and 40 in Week 2. Employee B records 46 in Week 1 and 34 in Week 2. Both show 80 total hours, but the weekly pattern is different. Whether and how overtime should be paid depends on the applicable rules and employer policy, so verify the threshold and classification independently from the arithmetic.

Partial weeks, leave and missing punches

A holiday, PTO day or missed punch can change the apparent weekly total. Enter only the work-time values that belong in this calculator and resolve missing or corrected punches from an approved record before relying on the total. The tool does not decide whether paid leave counts as hours worked for overtime or accrual purposes.

Rounding and payroll review

The rounding selector applies to each day's net worked minutes after the break deduction and before the calculator adds Week 1 and Week 2. Because rounding each day can differ from rounding only the final pay-period total, use the same documented convention as the source timekeeping record. Before printing, compare both weekly totals, break deductions, overtime setting, hourly rate and pay estimate with the authoritative payroll record.

The estimated gross pay is based only on the values entered here. It does not include taxes, deductions, bonuses, shift differentials, multiple pay rates or jurisdiction-specific payroll rules.
