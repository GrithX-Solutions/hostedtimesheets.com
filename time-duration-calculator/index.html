Quick answer

A time duration is the elapsed span between a starting point and an ending point.

Time duration = end date/time − start date/time

Example: 9:00 AM to 5:30 PM is 8 hours 30 minutes. With second precision, 9:00:15 AM to 5:30:45 PM is 8 hours 30 minutes 30 seconds, or about 8.5083 decimal hours.

How to calculate time duration

For a same-day span, convert each clock time to a common unit such as seconds after midnight, then subtract the start from the end. This calculator performs that arithmetic automatically and returns the result in human-readable time, decimal hours, total minutes and total seconds.

Time-only duration seconds = end seconds − start seconds

If the result is negative in Time only mode, the calculator adds 24 hours because the span crossed midnight. If start and end are identical, the duration is zero; the calculator does not assume a 24-hour span.

Example: overnight duration

Enter 10:00 PM as the start and 6:00 AM as the end. The end is earlier on the clock, so the calculator treats it as 6:00 AM the next day and returns 8 hours.

Date and time duration across multiple days

Switch to Date & time mode when the span includes specific calendar dates. The calculator combines the whole-day difference with the start and end clock times. It rejects an end date/time that occurs before the start instead of silently reversing the inputs.

Example: October 3 at 10:00 PM to October 5 at 12:30 AM is 1 day 2 hours 30 minutes, which equals 26.50 hours.

Seconds precision

The time inputs use one-second steps. This matters when you measure short tasks, media durations, tests, processing time or any interval where rounding to the nearest minute would hide useful information.

For example, 8:30:15 AM to 8:31:45 AM is 1 minute 30 seconds, 1.50 total minutes and 90 total seconds.

Subtracting a break

The optional break field subtracts whole break minutes from the gross duration. Leave it blank or enter 0 for no break. Negative and fractional break minutes are rejected instead of being silently changed. The calculator keeps the full duration visible and shows a separate net duration. If the break exceeds the gross duration, the net result stops at zero rather than becoming negative.

Net duration = max(0, gross duration − break minutes)

Time duration and elapsed time

Time duration and elapsed time describe the same underlying interval between two moments. The previous HostedTimesheets Elapsed Time Calculator has moved to this page so one canonical URL owns the broader time-duration intent while still answering elapsed-time questions.

What this calculator does not convert

The calculator treats entered dates and times as plain local values. It does not convert between cities, UTC offsets or daylight-saving rules. A DST transition can make real-world elapsed time differ from simple local-clock arithmetic, so use a timezone-aware system when that distinction matters.

Use the right time tool

Use this page when you know the start and end points and need the span between them. Use the Hours & Minutes Calculator when you already know durations and want to add or subtract them. Use the Work Hours Calculator for repeated work shifts and gross-pay estimates, or the Clock Out Time Calculator when you know when you started and want to plan the finish time.

Methodology and assumptions

Time only mode compares two clock times and treats an earlier end as the following day. Equal clock times mean zero duration. Date & time mode uses strict calendar dates with second-level clock values and requires the end to be at or after the start. Break minutes reduce only the net result. Calculations run locally in your browser and do not determine payroll, overtime, billing or employment rules.

Frequently asked questions

How many hours are between 9 AM and 5 PM?

There are 8 hours between 9:00 AM and 5:00 PM on the same day.

Can this time duration calculator include seconds?

Yes. The time inputs support seconds, and the results show the duration in hours, minutes and seconds plus total seconds.

How does the calculator handle an overnight time span?

In Time only mode, an end time earlier than the start time is treated as the next day. For example, 10:00 PM to 6:00 AM equals 8 hours.

Can I calculate duration between two dates?

Yes. Switch to Date & time mode, enter both dates and times, and the calculator includes the calendar-day difference in the result.

Can I subtract a break from the duration?

Yes. Enter break minutes to keep the gross duration visible while also calculating the net duration after the break.

Is time duration the same as elapsed time?

They describe the same span between a starting point and an ending point. This page targets time duration while also covering elapsed-time calculations.

Does this calculator convert between time zones?

No. It treats the entered dates and clock times as plain local values and does not convert cities, UTC offsets or daylight-saving transitions.

What happens if the start time and end time are the same?

In Time only mode, matching start and end times produce a zero duration. Use Date & time mode with consecutive dates when you need to represent a full 24-hour span.

Minutes to Decimal Hours

Time-duration math

Use one continuous timeline

Clock times repeat every 24 hours, but durations stay continuous. Track the day boundary before subtracting.

Crossing midnight adds a day

In Time only mode, 10:00 PM to 6:00 AM is not negative eight hours. The end occurs on the next day, so the continuous span is eight hours.

Seconds use the same base-60 rule as minutes

Sixty seconds make one minute and 60 minutes make one hour. A 30-second remainder is 0.5 minute, while 30 minutes is 0.5 hour. Keeping those conversions separate prevents decimal-time errors.

Date mode handles whole-day boundaries explicitly

Date & time mode counts the calendar-day difference first and then adds the clock-time difference. That method handles leap days because the entered dates are converted to UTC day numbers only for calendar arithmetic, not for timezone conversion.

Keep gross and net duration separate

Gross duration is the full span between the two endpoints. Net duration subtracts the optional break you enter. Showing both prevents a break deduction from hiding the original measured interval.
