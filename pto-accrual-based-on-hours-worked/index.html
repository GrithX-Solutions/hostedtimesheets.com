Hourly PTO formula

PTO Accrual Based on Hours Worked

For an hourly accrual policy, multiply hours worked by the PTO earned for each hour. This page shows the formula and examples for common work-hour totals.

Calculate PTO accrual

Quick answer

Multiply eligible hours worked by the PTO accrual rate in the policy. For example, 80 eligible hours × 0.0577 PTO hour per hour earns about 4.62 PTO hours before any policy-specific rounding, caps, or balance adjustments.

Per-hour PTO accrual formula

PTO earned = hours worked × PTO accrual rate

If the rate is 0.05 PTO hours for every hour worked, 80 work hours earn 4 PTO hours, 160 work hours earn 8 PTO hours, and 2,080 work hours earn 104 PTO hours before policy limits or adjustments.

Example: 80 hours worked

80 × 0.05 = 4 PTO hours

Example: 160 hours worked

160 × 0.05 = 8 PTO hours

How to include an existing balance

Add the newly earned PTO to the current balance and eligible carryover, then subtract PTO used. If the policy has a balance cap, apply that cap to the projected balance.

What rate should you use?

Use the accrual rate stated in the applicable employer or plan policy. This guide explains the arithmetic only and does not determine eligibility, legal requirements, forfeiture rules, or the correct policy rate.

Frequently asked questions

How much PTO do I earn per hour worked?

Multiply each hour worked by the accrual rate supplied by the applicable PTO policy.

Can I calculate vacation accrual the same way?

Yes, when the vacation policy uses a per-hour accrual rate, the arithmetic is the same.

Do overtime or paid-leave hours always earn PTO?

No. The policy determines which hours are eligible for accrual. Check whether it includes overtime, holidays, paid leave, training time, or other paid hours before applying the rate.

Hourly PTO method

Turn an annual PTO allowance into an hourly accrual rate

Hourly accrual works best when the policy defines which hours are eligible. The rate is only one part of the calculation; the eligible-hours rule matters just as much.

Hourly PTO rate = annual PTO allowance ÷ annual eligible work hours. If a plan grants 120 PTO hours across 2,080 eligible work hours, the mathematical rate is 0.057692 PTO hours per eligible hour. An 80-hour period at that rate earns about 4.62 PTO hours before rounding.

If the policy supplies the hourly rate directly, use that stated rate instead of deriving a new one from a yearly allowance.

Eligible hours are not always the same as paid hours

Do not assume every paid hour earns PTO. A policy may include regular worked hours but treat overtime, holidays, paid leave, unpaid leave, training or other categories differently. The calculator multiplies the hours you enter; it cannot determine which categories should be included for a particular employee.

Part-time and variable schedules

Hourly accrual is useful for employees whose schedules change because earned PTO can follow the actual eligible hours recorded. For example, at 0.05 PTO hours per eligible work hour, 30 hours earns 1.50 PTO hours while 42 eligible hours earns 2.10. This avoids assuming a fixed full-time schedule when the policy is based on actual hours.

Overtime and leave hours need a policy check

Some plans accrue PTO on overtime hours and others do not. The same is true for paid holiday or previously used PTO hours. Before adding those hours to the accrual base, check the plan language or payroll configuration. For a clean audit trail, keep the eligible-hours total separate from the employee's overall paid-hours total.

Apply balances, usage and caps after calculating new accrual

Calculate the new PTO earned first, then reconcile the balance: projected balance = opening balance + PTO earned + approved carryover − PTO used. If the policy has a cap, apply it according to the actual rule. Some systems stop future earning at the cap; others limit only the displayed balance.

Rounding can create small differences over a year

Keep enough decimal precision during intermediate calculations and round at the stage required by the payroll or leave system. Rounding every day's accrual too early can accumulate a different annual result than calculating at full precision and rounding once per pay period.

Hourly PTO audit checklist

Before finalizing a balance, verify the stated accrual rate, eligible-hours definition, hours imported from the time record, opening balance, PTO already used, carryover, cap and rounding convention. Hosted Timesheets provides arithmetic and planning estimates, not a determination of employment or leave-law requirements. Use the governing policy and authoritative payroll records for final employee balances.
