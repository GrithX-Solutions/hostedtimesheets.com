Clock Out Time Calculator

Find exactly when to clock out from your clock-in time, paid work target and unpaid breaks. The calculator shows 12-hour and 24-hour time and handles overnight finishes.

Quick answer

A clock out time calculator adds your paid work target and any unpaid breaks to your clock-in time. If you start at 8:30 AM, need 8 paid hours and take a 30-minute unpaid lunch, your projected clock-out time is 5:00 PM (17:00).

Clock-out time = clock-in time + paid work target + total unpaid breaks

The result is a planning calculation. Enter the schedule values that apply to you; the tool does not decide whether a break should be paid or unpaid.

How the clock-out calculation works

The calculator converts the clock-in time and each duration to minutes. It adds paid hours and paid minutes, then adds the unpaid lunch and any optional additional unpaid break. If the result passes midnight, it wraps the clock display and keeps a day-offset label.

Paid minutes = paid hours × 60 + paid minutes

Total unpaid break = lunch / break + additional unpaid break

Total span = paid minutes + total unpaid break

Projected clock-out = clock-in + total span

Worked example: eight paid hours with lunch

An eight-hour paid target is not always an eight-hour clock span. From 8:30 AM to 4:30 PM is eight elapsed clock hours, but a 30-minute unpaid lunch inside that span leaves only 7 hours 30 minutes of paid work. To complete 8 paid hours with that lunch, the projected finish is 5:00 PM.

Worked example: seven hours 30 minutes

For a 7-hour-30-minute paid target, enter 7 in paid hours and 30 in paid minutes. Starting at 9:00 AM with no unpaid break produces a 4:30 PM clock-out. Thirty minutes is half an hour; it is not 0.30 of a decimal hour.

Lunch plus another unpaid break

If you have more than one unpaid break, you do not need to add them mentally. Enter the main lunch or break in the first field and another unpaid break in the optional field. Starting at 8:00 AM with an 8-hour paid target, a 30-minute lunch and another 15-minute unpaid break produces a 4:45 PM clock-out.

Overnight clock-out times

Late shifts can finish on the following day. Starting at 10:00 PM and adding 8 paid hours with no unpaid break produces 6:00 AM the next day. The calculator shows both 12-hour and 24-hour time and labels the day offset explicitly.

The calculator can also display results more than one day later when the entered paid target and unpaid breaks extend beyond 24 hours. The inputs are intentionally bounded so obviously malformed values are rejected instead of silently changed.

Paid breaks versus unpaid breaks

Only enter break time that should extend the clock-out time without counting toward the paid-work target. If a break is already included in the paid target, do not add it again as unpaid time. The calculator does not classify breaks or determine which workplace rule applies.

Local clock arithmetic and daylight-saving time

This page uses a clock time but no calendar date or time zone. That keeps ordinary shift planning simple, but it also means daylight-saving transitions are not modeled. On a day when clocks move forward or backward, actual elapsed time can differ from a simple wall-clock calculation.

If you need date-aware elapsed time rather than a projected shift-end clock time, use the Time Duration Calculator.

Use the right calculator for the question

Use this page when you know when you clocked in and want to know when you can clock out after completing a paid target and unpaid breaks. If you already know both the start and end time and need the duration, use the Work Hours Calculator. For punch records across multiple days, use the Time Card Calculator or Weekly Timesheet Calculator.

Methodology and limits

Paid hours are whole hours from 0 through 72. Paid minutes are whole minutes from 0 through 59. Each unpaid-break field accepts whole minutes from 0 through 1,440; the additional-break field is optional and a blank value means zero. Invalid or out-of-range values are rejected and suppress the result instead of being clamped.

The calculator performs minute-level arithmetic from the values you enter. It does not apply payroll rounding, overtime thresholds, taxes, pay rates, time-zone offsets, daylight-saving rules, break laws or employer policies.

Frequently asked questions

What time should I clock out for an 8-hour shift?

Add 8 paid hours plus any unpaid break to your clock-in time. If you start at 8:30 AM and take a 30-minute unpaid lunch, you clock out at 5:00 PM.

Does an unpaid lunch change my clock-out time?

Yes. An unpaid break extends the time between clock-in and clock-out without adding paid work time. Enter 0 when no unpaid break should be added.

Can I add more than one unpaid break?

Yes. Use the main unpaid-break field for lunch and the optional additional-break field for another unpaid break. The calculator adds both to the paid-work target.

Can the clock-out time be after midnight?

Yes. The calculator carries the result past midnight automatically and labels a next-day or later finish.

Can I enter a 7 hour 30 minute shift?

Yes. Enter 7 paid hours and 30 paid minutes, then add any unpaid breaks separately.

Does this calculator account for daylight-saving time?

No. It uses local wall-clock arithmetic without a date or time zone. A daylight-saving transition can make real elapsed time differ from the displayed clock-time span.

Does this calculator decide whether my break is paid or unpaid?

No. Enter only break time you have already identified as unpaid. The calculator does not determine break entitlement, compensability, overtime eligibility or payroll rules.
